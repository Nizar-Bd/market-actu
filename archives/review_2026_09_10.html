US stocks fell further as a surge in oil and fuel prices lifted Treasury yields and dented margins for the corporate sector. The S&P 500 fell 0.6%, the Dow dropped 0.4%, and the Nasdaq 100 lost 1.3%. Meanwhile, Apple inched higher to pare yesterday's losses after it announced the release of a series of new products, including the foldable iPhone Duo.

The CAC 40 rose 0.3% to 8,179 on Thursday, rebounding from a near three-month low in the previous session as investors awaited the European Central Bank's interest rate decision later in the day. While the ECB is widely expected to deliver a 25 bps rate hike, investors remain focused on policymakers' guidance regarding the future path of monetary policy. Risk sentiment also remained fragile as oil prices stayed elevated after Iran signaled its readiness for a more intense conflict, while US President Donald Trump said the war was unlikely to end before the November midterm elections. Among individual stocks, L'Oréal (-0.3%), Dassault Systèmes (-1.1%), and Pernod Ricard (-1.0%) were among the biggest drags on the index. In contrast, TotalEnergies gained 0.6%, reaching its highest level since May, as higher crude prices continued to support energy shares.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 extended early losses, falling 0.7% to around 25,400 on Thursday afternoon, a level last seen in late July. Traders digested the ECB’s latest decision, with policymakers raising rates by 25 basis points as expected and warning of continued upside risks to inflation stemming from the Middle East conflict. The move fueled expectations of further tightening. In the meantime, oil prices surged to fresh highs, as escalating US-Iran hostilities stoked fears of a prolonged conflict. Consumer cyclicals, software and technology stocks, and industrials faced the heaviest losses. Hochtief led losses among stocks, dropping 5.1%, with Adidas, Zalando and Siemens Energy also posting steep declines. SAP slipped 2.7%, as traders were cautious ahead of the quarterly earnings reports from the two IT and software giants, Oracle and Adobe. On the gainers side, shares of Deutsche Borse (1.9%), Hannover Ruck (1.8%) and Munchener Ruck (1.5%) stood out.

The FTSE 100 fell around 0.5% to a seven-week low in afternoon trading on Thursday, as Brent crude surged above $105 a barrel after Iran warned it was prepared for a more intense conflict. Mining stocks came under heavy pressure, with Antofagasta and Anglo American falling more than 5%, while Endeavour, Glencore and Rio Tinto each declined by more than 3%. Associated British Foods was also among the biggest losers, dropping over 9% after Primark sales came in weaker than expected and the group outlined plans to demerge the retailer by December 2027. Energy stocks provided some support, with BP gaining 2% and Shell rising 1.8% as higher oil prices boosted the sector. Meanwhile, the European Central Bank raised interest rates by 25 basis points, in line with market expectations. The ECB also lifted its inflation forecasts and warned that price growth is likely to remain above its 2% target for an extended period.

European stocks moved lower on Thursday, with both the STOXX 50 and STOXX 600 falling 0.3%, reversing early opening gains and extending losses of more than 1% in the previous session.
