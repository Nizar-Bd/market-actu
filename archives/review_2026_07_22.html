The CAC 40 rose 0.3% to 8,385 on Wednesday, extending gains from the previous session and reaching its highest level in a week as investors focused on corporate earnings and updates while keeping a close eye on escalating tensions in the Middle East. As the US carried out its eleventh consecutive day of strikes on Iran, President Donald Trump ruled out near-term negotiations with Tehran and vowed retaliation if Iran-backed Houthi rebels disrupt shipping in the Red Sea. On the corporate front, Airbus climbed 5.1% to its highest level in six months after forecasting adjusted EBIT of €12–13 billion by 2029, reaffirming its 2026 guidance, announcing a €5 billion share buyback program, and highlighting plans to accelerate production across its A220, A320, A330, and A350 families. In contrast, luxury stocks lagged the broader market as investors continued to rotate toward cyclical sectors, with LVMH slipping 1.0% and Hermès declining 1.2%.

Meanwhile, Gea advanced over 5% after the industrial equipment maker raised its full-year outlook following a stronger-than-expected quarterly performance. The FTSE 100 edged higher on Wednesday as investors balanced softer-than-expected UK inflation against another rise in oil prices, with commodity-related stocks leading gains. UK annual inflation slowed to 2.6% in June from 2.8% in May, below market expectations of 2.7% and the lowest reading since March 2025, helped by lower petrol prices. Meanwhile, Brent crude climbed to its highest level in more than a month after President Donald Trump warned of further strikes on Iran and vowed retaliation if Iran-backed Houthi forces disrupted shipping in the Red Sea. Higher oil prices supported energy shares, with Shell and BP gaining 0.5% and 1%, respectively. Mining stocks also advanced, with Endeavour Mining and Fresnillo rising 2.5% and 1%, while Fresnillo reaffirmed it remains on track to meet its production guidance. Banks and defence stocks also traded higher, adding to the index's modest gains.
