US stocks rose on Friday as gains in major technology stocks lifted the market in a volatile session. The S&P 500 rose 0.7%, the Nasdaq 100 gained 0.6%, and the Dow added 277 points. Amazon jumped 15.3% on strong cloud growth. Optimism around the AI trade boosted software and hyperscalers, with Alphabet gaining 6.9%, Microsoft adding 3%, and Meta rising 3.3%. Those gains outweighed a decline in Apple (-7.3%), after chip shortages raised costs and reduced June-quarter production, weighing on earnings. Elsewhere, broader sectors were weaker, with Netflix down 2% and Eli Lilly losing 0.6%. ExxonMobil fell 1% as limited refinery capacity prevented the oil major from fully benefiting from higher oil prices last quarter. The Dow and the S&P 500 each gained about 1% for the week, while the Nasdaq advanced roughly 1.6%. For July, however, the S&P 500 slipped 0.1% and the Nasdaq declined 3.2%. The Dow bucked the trend, edging up 0.3% to record its fourth straight monthly gain.

The CAC 40 gained 0.3% to close at 8,510 on Friday as investors assessed another round of corporate earnings and business updates. Engie surged 5.3% after reporting strong first-half results and raising its 2026 guidance. Crédit Agricole gained 2.5% after beating second-quarter expectations, while Saint-Gobain jumped 7% as the company returned to double-digit sales growth in the second quarter and reaffirmed its 2026 outlook. TotalEnergies added 0.8% as oil prices extended gains. Luxury stocks were mixed as persistent Middle East tensions weighed on risk sentiment. LVMH rose 0.4%, while L'Oréal fell 1.8% and Hermès lost 1.1%. On the data front, France's annual inflation rate accelerated to 2.1% in July from 1.8% in June, reinforcing the case for a more cautious ECB easing cycle. Meanwhile, producer prices fell 0.6% month-over-month in June after a revised 0.2% decline in May.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 edged up on the final trading session of July, reaching its highest level since the record peak set earlier this month and recording a monthly gain of more than 2%. The advance was driven by a strong rebound in technology stocks, broadly upbeat corporate earnings, and a continued sharp decline in oil prices, despite persistent geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. Chipmaker Infineon led the benchmark with a gain of more than 4%, followed by Hochtief, which advanced 2.7%. On the downside, Siemens Healthineers fell 1% after lowering its fiscal 2025/26 revenue guidance due to continued weakness in its diagnostics business. Elsewhere, defense electronics company Hensoldt reported that order intake more than doubled year over year, sportswear manufacturer Puma reaffirmed its full-year guidance despite weaker demand, and wafer producer Siltronic posted strong quarterly results.

The FTSE 100 edged lower on Friday due to weakness in heavyweight consumer goods, healthcare, and banking stocks. Unilever dropped more than 2%, while AstraZeneca and GSK each declined around 0.5%. Major lenders HSBC Holdings and Lloyds Banking also fell. IAG fell more than 1% after reporting second-quarter revenue below expectations, reflecting the impact of ongoing disruption from the Middle East conflict on air travel. In contrast, NatWest surged more than 4% after raising its full-year guidance and indicating it could launch additional share buybacks. Sainsbury's gained around 2% after announcing the sale of its Argos business. Energy stocks also advanced, with BP and Shell rising between 1.3% and 1.8% as oil prices extended their sharp monthly rally of more than 20%. Despite Friday's decline, the FTSE 100 finished the week up more than 1% and posted a monthly gain of roughly 3.5%, its strongest performance since February.
