The CAC 40 gained 0.3% to 8,481 on Wednesday, extending its winning streak to a fourth consecutive session and reaching its highest level in over three weeks, as strong earnings reports and corporate updates boosted investor sentiment. Luxury stocks led the advance, with LVMH climbing 3.1% after reporting quarterly sales broadly in line with expectations, while Kering surged 11.3% to its highest level since early February after Gucci delivered better-than-expected sales. Meanwhile, Legrand added 1.7% after raising its sales guidance, citing continued robust investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure. On the downside, Hermès fell 2.5% despite reporting sales growth that met market forecasts, as investors focused on the lack of a meaningful recovery in China. Danone also dropped 3.7% after reporting higher second-quarter sales, as the increase was driven primarily by price hikes rather than stronger volumes.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 climbed above the 25,500 mark on Wednesday, recovering from early volatility to reach its highest level since July 6, as a strong batch of corporate earnings helped offset renewed geopolitical tensions. Sentiment remained cautious, however, after fresh Iranian attacks lifted oil and gas prices, while investors also awaited the Federal Reserve's policy decision and earnings from major US technology companies. Among individual stocks, Rheinmetall surged nearly 7% after reporting sharp increases in revenue and operating profit, driven by strong defense demand. BASF gained 3.8% after announcing a new share buyback program, while Siemens Healthineers rose 3.5% as upbeat results from US peer GE Healthcare boosted the sector. European equities traded lower in choppy session on Wednesday, weighed down by technology stocks ahead of the US Federal Reserve's policy decision later in the day, upcoming earnings from major US tech companies, and ongoing tensions in the Middle East. The STOXX 50 fell 0.5%, while the broader STOXX 600 lost 0.3%. Tech stocks led the declines, with ASM International dropping more than 5% despite an upbeat outlook. Sentiment was also hurt after SK Hynix's record quarterly results failed to meet lofty expectations, raising concerns over AI spending. Energy stocks outperformed as oil prices rebounded on renewed Middle East tensions. Among individual movers, Kering surged 12% after Gucci's sales beat expectations, Sopra Steria jumped 13% on raised guidance, Glencore gained 3% after stronger copper output, while UBS and Deutsche Bank rose on solid earnings. Aberdeen fell 5% after reporting larger-than-expected first-half fund outflows.
