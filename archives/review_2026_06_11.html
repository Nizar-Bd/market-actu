US equity indices slightly rebounded from one-month lows on Thursday with support from the tech sector, offsetting risks of higher interest rates. The S&P 500, Dow, and Nasdaq rose up to 0.5%. The CAC 40 rose 0.6% to 8,210 on Thursday, rebounding from the previous session, as investors turned their attention to the European Central Bank's policy decision due later today. The central bank is anticipated to deliver a 25 bps hike, marking its first interest rate increase since 2023. Policymakers seek to contain inflationary pressures exacerbated by surging energy costs linked to the conflict involving Iran. Markets will also closely scrutinize the ECB's signals on the future path of monetary policy. Geopolitical risks remained elevated in the Middle East after the US military struck multiple targets in Iran for a second straight day. President Trump accused Tehran of delaying negotiations over an interim peace deal and warned of further military action if no agreement is reached. Luxury stocks led gains, with LVMH up 1% and Hermès rising 0.9%. Other notable performers included TotalEnergies (1.1%), STMicroelectronics (2.6%), and Société Générale (1.2%).

The DAX 40 pared some initial gains to trade slightly down around 24,120 on Thursday afternoon, as traders reacted to the latest escalation in the Middle East tensions and the ECB's policy decision outcome. The US president has warned that his military will attack Iran this Thursday evening and will soon take control of the country's oil infrastructure as well as its oil and gas markets. Meanwhile, the ECB unsurprisingly raised its key interest rates, while signaling a more challenging outlook by raising inflation forecasts and lowering growth projections. Among equities, SAP (-4.5%) remained at the bottom of the index, pressured by Oracle's slide following post-results spending plans. Deutsche Telekom shares 3% following a Wall Street Journal report that CEO Tim Höttges is pursuing a merger between the German telecom group and its US subsidiary, T-Mobile US. On the flip side, Siemens Energy (3%), RWE (2.6%) and Bayer (2.2%) were the standout performers.

The FTSE 100 traded higher on Thursday, extending the previous session’s 0.3% gain, supported by strength in financial and mining stocks. Stocks in Europe remained higher on Thursday, with both the STOXX 50 and the STOXX 600 advancing around 0.8%, as investors largely shrugged off developments in the Middle East and the ECB's latest policy decision. President Trump vowed additional strikes on Iran and threatened to target the country's energy infrastructure. Meanwhile, the ECB raised interest rates by 25bps while revising its inflation forecasts higher and lowering its growth projections. However, these moves had been broadly priced in by investors. The technology sector was among the top gainers, supported by a global rebound in semiconductor stocks following recent weakness in the industry. ASML Holding gained 2.6%, Infineon Technologies advanced 0.8%, and STMicroelectronics jumped 4%. Luxury goods makers also traded higher, with LVMH rising 1.9% and Hermès adding 0.9%. Meanwhile, Hugo Boss surged more than 9% after its largest shareholder, Frasers Group, launched a $2 billion takeover bid for the fashion house.
