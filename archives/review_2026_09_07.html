US stocks closed mostly lower on Friday after stronger-than-expected labor market data gave the Fed more room to raise interest rates. The S&P 500 lost 0.4%, the Dow shed 272 points, while the Nasdaq added 0.2%. Nonfarm payrolls increased by 162K, above expectations for a gain of just over 50K jobs. Credit-sensitive sectors pulled back after the data as yields rebounded. Alphabet shed 1.1%, Microsoft lost 2%, Tesla tumbled 5.9%, and Palantir shed 4.5%, while JPMorgan retreated 0.9% and Visa fell 1%. Still, chipmakers rose after underperforming earlier in the week, helping to support the Nasdaq. Nvidia gained 0.8%, Micron rose 6.1%, Intel added 4.5%, and Marvell advanced 7% amid signs of optimism surrounding OpenAI’s new GPT model. Sandisk soared 11.9%, and AMD added 4.7%. On the week, the Dow dropped 0.3% while the S&P 500 added 0.1% and the Nasdaq rose 0.4%. The US stock market will be closed Monday for the Labor Day holiday.

The CAC 40 reversed early losses to close about 0.3% up at 8,306 on Monday, its highest level in a week and ranking among the strongest performers across Europe. Technology provided the main support, led by STMicroelectronics (+4%) following a renewed enthusiasm for artificial intelligence after the launch of GPT-6 Astra by OpenAI, the startup's " world's smartest " model. Schneider Electric and Legrand also advanced solidly, rising 2.7% and 2.4%, respectively. TotalEnergies gained 1.5%, supported by higher oil prices and news that it had completed several major contractual and commercial milestones for the Papua LNG project. Meanwhile, investors remained wary as geopolitical tensions drove energy prices higher, while expectations of additional rate hikes and mounting political uncertainty in Europe reinforced caution. The ECB is widely expected to deliver a 25-bps rate hike on Thursday, while markets are also pricing in a Fed hike next week.

The DAX 40 edged down 0.3% to around 25,960 on Monday, as investors remained cautious amid escalating Middle East tensions and ahead of this week’s key ECB decision. Markets also digested the implications of the AfD’s victory in Saxony-Anhalt, which dealt a major blow to Chancellor Friedrich Merz’s CDU. Meanwhile, the ECB is widely expected to raise rates by 25 bps to 2.5% on Thursday. With Eurozone inflation already above 3% and well above the ECB’s 2% target, the bigger market question is whether Christine Lagarde signals that further tightening could be needed. Qiagen NV and SAP posted the biggest losses among stocks, falling 2.7% and 2.5%, respectively. Vonovia dropped 2% after Goldman Sachs cut its rating from Buy to Neutral and slashed its price target from €29.50 to €21.20, signaling a more cautious view on the property group’s outlook. On the flip side, chipmaker Infineon Technologies (3.2%) and AI-related stocks Siemens Energy (2.3%) and Hochtief (2%) stood out.

Meanwhile, domestic data showed UK house prices fell 0.2% in August, pushing annual prices into negative territory for the first time since November 2023, according to Lloyds.

European stock indices closed with small movements on Monday as markets continued to assess how the corporate sector will fare against higher interest rates, with the threat of higher budget deficits and political uncertainty lifting sovereign yields. The Euro STOXX 50 inched up by 0.1% to 6,399 and the STOXX Europe 600 was unchanged at 649.8. The German far-right AfD party won state elections in Saxony-Anhalt over the weekend, the party's first major victory, to extend the momentum of political fragmentation in Europe. Bund yields surged with the outlook of wider deficits combined with inflationary risks from soaring natural gas prices, as the European Central Bank is due to deliver its second rate hike of the year this week. Insurance stocks tumbled, with Munich Re down 3% while Allianz fell nearly 1%. Meanwhile, ASML gained 2.3% and Infineon soared 6.4% as strong signals by Korean chipmakers supported the sector.
