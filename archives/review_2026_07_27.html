US stocks rose on Monday, erasing the losses from last week as drop in energy prices softened the deteriorating macroeconomic backdrop. The S&P 500 gained 0.8%, while both the Dow and the Nasdaq 100 rose over 1%. The US paused strikes against Iran over the weekend and benchmark fuel prices eased on hopes that energy exports from the Middle East could return. The consequent drop in yields loosened financial conditions to reflect almost null expectations the Federal Reserve may deliver a rate hike this week, pushing back eventual tightening to September. Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, and Apple rose up to 2% ahead of their earnings in the coming days. Nvidia underperformed with a 0.5% loss after a $500 billion AI memory chip supply deal from SK Hynix and guaranteeing $250 billion for OpenAI to lease computing from a data center project, reigniting concerns that circular deals in AI computing could trigger contagion and unwind the AI trade should capex growth be halted.

The CAC 40 rose 0.7% to 8,428 on Monday, extending last week's gains as signs of de-escalation in the Middle East boosted market sentiment. The US and Iran halted hostilities following nearly two weeks of retaliatory attacks that had effectively derailed the June ceasefire. At the same time, Iranian and Omani officials discussed efforts to ensure the safe passage of vessels through the Strait of Hormuz, easing concerns over potential supply disruptions. Luxury stocks spearheaded the advance, with LVMH gaining 1.2% ahead of its first-half results release later today, while Hermès and Kering rose 2.3% and 2.4%, respectively. Other notable gainers included Airbus (+1.5%), Safran (+2.9%), BNP Paribas (+1.4%), and EssilorLuxottica (+2.1%). On the downside, TotalEnergies was among the few laggards, falling 2.4% as crude oil prices tumbled amid easing fears of supply disruptions in the Middle East.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 rose more than 1% to 25,440 on Monday, extending last week's 1.1% gain, as easing US-Iran tensions and lower oil and bond yields lifted risk sentiment ahead of a busy week of central bank meetings and earnings from major technology companies. Brent crude retreated from two-month highs, easing inflation concerns and prompting investors to scale back expectations for further policy tightening. Travel stocks led the gains on hopes that lower fuel costs will support margins and consumer spending, with Lufthansa jumping more than 3%. Chipmaker Infineon added 0.9% after the strong stock market debut of Chinese semiconductor firm CXMT, while construction group Hochtief fell 2.3% ahead of its quarterly earnings release after the close. Investors now await policy decisions from the US Federal Reserve and the Bank of England later this week, along with a fresh batch of European inflation data, for further clues on the interest rate outlook.

The FTSE 100 traded 0.5% higher on Monday to its highest level since early March, although it underperformed other major European indexes as declines in heavyweight energy stocks limited gains. Sentiment improved after the US paused its air campaign against Iran for a second consecutive night, while Tehran refrained from retaliatory strikes and continued discussions with Oman over the Strait of Hormuz. The easing geopolitical tensions pushed Brent crude back to below $90 per barrel, weighing on oil producers. Shell fell by around 1.5% and BP lost about 3.5%. On the other hand, AstraZeneca added 1.7% after reporting second-quarter earnings above expectations and reaffirmed its full-year guidance and 2030 targets. Also, GSK added 1.5%, Rolls-Royce climbed 1%, and HSBC rose 0.6%.

European stocks traded higher on Monday, with the STOXX 50 gaining 1.2% and the STOXX 600 advancing 0.8%. Investor sentiment improved after the US and Iran paused strikes, while Iranian and Omani officials met to discuss measures to ensure the safe passage of shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. These developments eased concerns over the risk of further supply disruptions, triggering a sharp decline in oil prices. The energy sector was the session's weakest performer as lower crude prices weighed on oil and gas stocks, while all other major sectors traded in positive territory. Travel and leisure gained sharply, including Lufthansa (2.9%) and IAG (2.9%). SAP (5%) and Inditex (3%) also booked strong gains. Investors also digested a fresh batch of corporate earnings. AstraZeneca rose 1.7% after reporting better-than-expected profits and reaffirming its earnings guidance for 2026. LVMH gained 1.3% ahead of the release of its first-half results later in the day.
