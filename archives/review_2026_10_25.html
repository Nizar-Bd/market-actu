The CAC 40 fell 0.4% to close at 7,783 on Friday ahead of Moody’s review of France’s sovereign credit rating. Moody’s rates France Aa3, its fourth-highest grade, with a negative outlook, and a downgrade is widely expected. The 10-year government bond yield surged to a 24-year high as concerns grew over fragile public finances, rising borrowing costs and mounting social unrest. The minority government faces difficulties passing its budget and reducing the deficit amid parliamentary divisions, widespread protests and uncertainty ahead of the presidential election. Meanwhile, heavyweight companies reported Q3 results this week. Hermès fell after reporting revenue below expectations, while Kering plunged 16.6% following a sales decline. Safran sank 10% despite strong results. In contrast, L’Oréal climbed 5.6% after third-quarter sales beat expectations. On the week, the CAC 40 gained 0.2%.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 fell about 0.8% to close at 25,152 on Friday, its lowest level since October 9 and extending previous session's losses. The continued rise in oil prices amid geopolitical tensions has heightened fears of inflationary pressures, while renewed uncertainty surrounding AI and the instability in France further weighed on investor sentiment. On the data front, flash PMI figures showed that Germany’s private sector continued to expand in October, although growth slowed more than expected. Most sectors recorded declines, particularly telecom, tech, industrials and autos. MTU Aero Engines was the worst performer, slipping nearly 5%, trailing the mixed results of its competitor Safran. Deutsche Telekom followed closely, down 3.2%, on renewed concerns about US competition from SpaceX. Siemens Energy, Infineon Technologies, RWE and Merck also declined sharply, each falling more than 2%. For the week, the index lost around 1.8%.
