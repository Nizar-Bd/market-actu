US stocks moved higher on Friday, with the S&P 500 gaining 0.4%, the Nasdaq rising 0.6% and the Dow Jones advancing 107 points. The gains were driven by a rebound in tech shares following the previous session’s sell-off. Sentiment was supported by reports that OpenAI expects to reach or exceed $70 billion in annualized revenue by year-end, easing concerns that sales were falling short ofprojections. Meanwhile, lower oil prices helped alleviate some pressure on the bond market. Consumer discretionary and real estate stocks were also among the strongest performers. Tesla jumped 2.7% as Elon Musk made it clear who will own and operate Terafab. On the other hand, Apple shares fell 2.5% after reports it told some suppliers to cut production of components for iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max models. Delta Air Lines was also down 3% after missing on earnings and cutting its outlook. On the week, both the Dow and the Nasdaq are little changed while the S&P 500 is up 0.6% so far.

The CAC 40 rose 0.9% to close at 7,803, recovering from two consecutive sessions of losses as markets pared back expectations for ECB rate hikes. Money markets now price in a deposit rate of 2.72% by December, up from the current 2.50%, and 3.20% by late 2027. Gains were broad-based, with BNP Paribas up 0.8%, Safran rising 1.7% and Schneider Electric adding 2%. Luxury stocks also advanced, with Hermès up 1.6% and Kering gaining 2.3%. Domestically, French markets have faced mounting pressure as investors weigh the country’s debt burden, fiscal deficit and political uncertainty ahead of the 2027 presidential election.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 gained ground to close about 1.1% firmer at 25,102 on Friday, reversing a two-day decline that sent the index to the lowest in two months. Investor sentiment improved after President Trump ruled out a US attack on Iran before the November midterm elections, citing progress in talks to end the conflict. His remarks pushed oil prices lower and eased pressure on the bond market. Meanwhile, caution prevailed amid persistent tensions in the Gulf. Software giant SAP was the top performer, rising 3.8%, followed by Zalando (3.2%) and Bayer (3.2%). Siemens, BMW, Deutsche Post and Adidas, Fresenius Medical Care and Siemens Energy also advanced firmly, each adding over 2%. On the downside, Deutsche Telekom plunged nearly 8% as SpaceX’s spectrum acquisition raised concerns over increased competition for T-Mobile US, in which it holds a 54% stake. For the week, the index lost about 0.5%.

The UK's FTSE 100 rose further to close about 1.1% up at 10,552 on Friday, its highest since September 30, halting a two-day losing streak. Sentiment improved as oil prices retreated, easing pressure on bond yields, after Trump signalled a pause in potential US military action against Iran ahead of next month's midterm elections and described ongoing talks as “productive”. Software-related stocks were among the best performers, with Relx, Sage Group and Experian rising 4.8%, 4.3% and 4.2%, respectively. Mining stocks also advanced strongly, with Antofagasta surging over 5%, amid firmer prices of copper. Fresnillo rose more than 3%, tracking gains in silver and gold. On the flip side, telecom stocks including Airtel Africa (-6.3%), Vodafone (-4.9%) and BT Group (-2.4%) faced pressure after SpaceX agreed to acquire spectrum to strengthen Starlink Mobile’s US ambitions. For the week, the FTSE 100 rose by 0.9%.

European stocks closed higher on Friday, with the STOXX 50 gaining 0.7% and STOXX 600 adding 1% as easing oil prices and bond yields lifted market sentiment. US President Donald Trump said Washington would not launch an attack on Iran before the November midterm elections, citing productive talks aimed at ending the conflict. Meanwhile, AI sentiment also improved following reports that OpenAI expected its annualized revenue to reach or exceed $70 billion by year-end. Telecom stocks, however, came under pressure. Deutsche Telekom fell 7% after SpaceX struck a deal to acquire a nationwide portfolio of low-band spectrum, intensifying competition with traditional wireless operators in the US. Deutsche Telekom owns a 54% stake in T-Mobile US. Vodafone dropped 3%, while Orange and Telefónica each declined 2%. Meanwhile, LSEG IBES data showed expectations for European Q3 earnings growth rising to 21%, up from 19.4% a week earlier, ahead of the start of the earnings season.
