The CAC 40 rose 0.6% to 7,864 on Monday, recovering from losses in the previous week following US President Donald Trump’s remarks on an “energy ceasefire.” Oil prices fell after Trump said Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an immediate ceasefire targeting energy infrastructure, while Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Kyiv supports the truce but is “awaiting details.” However, lingering concerns over escalating tensions in the Middle East continued to temper risk appetite after a deadly attack on Riyadh’s airport prompted Trump to consider joining Saudi Arabia in retaliatory action against Houthi militants in Yemen. On the corporate front, LVMH rose 0.9% ahead of its quarterly earnings report, while Hermès and Kering each gained 1.6%. Financial stocks also advanced, led by Société Générale (1.6%) and BNP Paribas (1%).

Frankfurt’s DAX 40 closed 0.8% higher at 25,268 on Monday, extending gains from the previous session amid broad-based advances across sectors. Automakers rose after European manufacturers backed a plan to cap imports of Chinese hybrid vehicles, which have posed increasing competition for the industry. BMW gained 1.8%, while Mercedes-Benz advanced 0.6%. Volkswagen rose 0.7% after reporting a 14.3% decline in global deliveries in the third quarter, its sharpest drop in more than four years. Defense Rheinmetall climbed 3.2%. Utilities RWE (3.5%) and E.ON (0.7%) also advanced after Chancellor Friedrich Merz backed using any available budgetary leeway to reduce electricity taxes. Financial stocks moved higher as Deutsche Bank advanced 2.1% amid progress toward a possible settlement related to Malaysia’s 1MDB financial scandal. Commerzbank added 0.6%. Tech and industrial shares also advanced, led by Siemens (4.4%) and Siemens Energy (3%).

The FTSE 100 climbed 1% on Monday, following a 1.1% rise in the previous session, with banking stocks providing the strongest support. Lloyds Banking Group rose more than 2%, while HSBC gained nearly 2%, followed by Standard Chartered at 1.8%, Barclays at 1.5% and NatWest at 1.4%. HSBC also attracted attention after reports that it plans to place greater emphasis on performance-related bonuses rather than fixed salaries. Oil-related shares advanced as crude prices strengthened amid renewed geopolitical uncertainty. Shell gained 1% as concerns over attacks in Ukraine and Saudi Arabia raised questions about regional energy security. Unilever also added more than 1%, contributing to the broader market’s gains. However, Informa fell more than 1% ahead of its Global Health Exhibition in Riyadh later this month. The decline followed Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia’s main airport over the weekend and new UK travel warnings for parts of the country.

European stocks closed higher for a second session on Monday, tracking the positive momentum for most sectors in US equity markets on upbeat earnings projections. Sell-side firms signaled that global earnings growth is expected to remain robust ahead of the incoming results-sharing season, supporting most sectors despite the elevated yields backdrop. LVMH added 1.2% before releasing its third-quarter revenues, while Hermes rose over 2% to consolidate a strong session for luxury brands. Meanwhile, banks enjoyed some respite with Santander, BBVA, and ING adding close to 2%. On the other hand, ASML fell 1.5% amid a broad pullback for companies in AI infrastructure. The Eurozone's largest economy is due to report results this week.
