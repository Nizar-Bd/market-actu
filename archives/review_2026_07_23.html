The CAC 40 fell more than 1% to around 8,345 on Thursday, reversing the previous session’s gains as escalating geopolitical tensions dampened risk appetite amid a busy slate of corporate earnings and updates. President Trump warned that the US would strike Iranian infrastructure in response to attacks on vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz, while Tehran threatened retaliation against regional energy assets. Adding to concerns, Yemen’s Houthi rebels claimed responsibility for attacks on two Saudi tankers. Among corporate movers, STMicroelectronics plunged 14.3% after issuing a Q3 sales forecast below market expectations, while BNP Paribas slipped 1.1% despite reporting upbeat Q2 results. In contrast, Thales added 1.8% after reporting a surge in first-half orders, and Dassault Aviation surged 7.5% after posting strong first-half earnings and reaffirming its 2026 sales outlook. Moreover, TotalEnergies climbed 2% after reporting a 68% jump in adjusted net income to $6.03 billion.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 fell 0.9% to 24,930 on Thursday, retreating from a one-week high after the European Central Bank left interest rates unchanged, as expected. The ECB reiterated that the outlook for energy prices remains broadly in line with its June projections but warned that uncertainty remains elevated and that the full inflationary impact of the recent energy shock has yet to materialize. Policymakers said they will continue to monitor the shock's magnitude, duration, and potential indirect and second-round effects. Meanwhile, rising oil prices amid the ongoing Iran conflict kept inflation concerns in focus, while mixed US corporate earnings weighed on sentiment despite stronger-than-expected results from Alphabet. Among individual stocks, SAP fell 0.6% ahead of its second-quarter earnings, Deutsche Börse was little changed after its Investment Management Solutions unit narrowly missed estimates, and Sartorius slipped 1% despite reporting higher first-half revenue.

European stocks extended their losses on Thursday, with the STOXX 50 falling 1.1% and the STOXX 600 down 0.9%, as investors digested a fresh batch of corporate earnings while surging oil prices and escalating hostilities in the Middle East continued to weigh on sentiment. As expected, the ECB left interest rates unchanged. STMicroelectronics tumbled more than 16%, making it the worst performer in the STOXX 600 after missing profit expectations. Nestlé dropped 7% after announcing an agreement to sell its stake in its Waters business to private equity firm Platinum Equity. UniCredit declined 4.4% despite reporting a 6.6% year-on-year increase in revenue and reaffirming its goal of acquiring Commerzbank in the fourth quarter. BNP Paribas also fell 2.1%, despite posting better-than-expected profit and revenue. On the upside, TotalEnergies gained 2.9% after reporting its strongest quarterly profit in three years, while Dassault Aviation surged 8.3% following stronger first-half earnings.
