US stock indices closed mixed on Friday as Treasury yields rebounded, rekindling concerns about a deteriorating macroeconomic backdrop. The S&P 500 inched 0.2% higher and the Dow inched 95 points lower, while the Nasdaq added 0.7%. The 10-year yield rebounded as persistent uncertainty over Middle Eastern oil supplies drove fuel and natural gas prices higher. The threat of energy-driven inflation has affected underlying consumer prices, prompting the Fed to hike rates this week. Most FOMC members saw the need for another hike. Credit-sensitive sectors fell, with banks and asset managers posting losses. Bank of America lost 0.8%, while Goldman Sachs fell 1%. AI hyperscalers were mostly lower amid their debt issuance spree to fund capital expenditures. Meta shed 2.4%, while Oracle lost 2%. Chipmakers were mostly higher, with Broadcom up 3% and Micron rising 3.9%, offsetting losses in the Nasdaq. On the week, the Dow shed 733 points, the Nasdaq rose 2.6% and the S&P 500 gained 0.7%.

The CAC 40 fell 1.5% to close at 8,065 on Friday, retreating after two consecutive sessions of gains as oil prices pared losses. The oil rally resumed after satellite imagery and industry sources indicated that three pumping stations serving Saudi Arabia’s vital East-West Pipeline were damaged in last week’s attack, one more than previously assessed, while the repair timeline remains uncertain. Luxury stocks fell amid higher risk aversion, with Hermes losing 3.2%. French bond yields moved higher amid concerns over energy-driven inflationary pressures. Financials also declined, with BNP Paribas down 2.9%. Meanwhile, the Fed and the BoJ raised rates earlier this week, while the BoE left interest rates unchanged. Still, traders pared back bets on further ECB tightening despite the Fed’s more hawkish stance, as concerns that persistently high energy prices could weigh on economic growth and eventually temper inflationary pressures. The CAC 40 shed 1.4% on the week.

Frankfurt’s DAX 40 closed about 1.6% down at 25,296 on Friday, its lowest since late July, as caution returned to markets. Trading was also influenced by the so-called “quadruple witching” day, when several major derivatives contracts expire simultaneously. Meanwhile, investors eyed developments in the Middle East ahead of next week’s summit between US President Trump and Gulf-state leaders. Locally, attention turns to state elections in Berlin and Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania on Sept. 20, in another test to Chancellor Friedrich Merz’s leadership following the AfD’s strong showing in Saxony-Anhalt. Almost all sectors fell, led by autos, telecoms and banks, while techs advanced. Volkswagen, Mercedes-Benz Group and BMW were among the worst performers, falling 5.5%, 4.5% and 4.2%, respectively. Deutsche Telekom dropped 4.1%, following a negative analyst report for its French competitor Orange. On the upside, Infineon Technologies gained 2.7%. For the week, the index posted a 1.1% loss.

The FTSE 100 fell 1.5% on Friday, retreating after two consecutive sessions of gains, as investors weighed central-bank policy decisions and lower oil prices. Banks led the decline, with HSBC down more than 1.5%, while Barclays and Lloyds Banking Group each fell around 2.5%. Shell and BP dropped 0.9% and 1.1%, respectively, while Unilever lost around 0.5% and Rolls-Royce more than 2%. Mining stocks were also under pressure, with Rio Tinto down 1.7% and Glencore falling 4%. The Bank of England kept interest rates unchanged on Thursday but warned that a prolonged Middle East conflict could require tighter policy, while the Federal Reserve and Bank of Japan raised rates this week and indicated further tightening could follow. On the data front, UK retail sales rose 0.5% in August, beating expectations for a 0.2% decline. For the week, the UK index was little changed.

European stocks closed sharply lower on Friday, tracking a negative session for global stocks as a worsening macroeconomic backdrop threatened earnings projections. The Euro STOXX 50 fell 1.5% to 6,229, its lowest in nearly two months, and the STOXX Europe 600 fell 1.2% to 635.3. Natural gas and fuel prices rebounded amid signs of escalation in the Iran war and Saudi Arabia's warning that it does not have enough oil supply for European refineries next month. Sovereign yields rose sharply amid consolidated bets that the European Central Bank will continue to raise interest rates. Credit sensitive sectors hence booked losses, with Santander, BNP Paribas, and Deutsche Bank slipping over 3% each. German automakers also dropped, with Mercedes Benz, Volkswagen, and BMW sliding 5% each. On the other hand, global respite for AI infrastructure stocks lifted ASML and Infineon by 1.5% and 2.5%, respectively.
