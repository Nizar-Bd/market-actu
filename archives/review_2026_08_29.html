The US stock indices closed lower on Friday as chipmakers shares trimmed the previous session’s gains and a more hawkish tone from Fed Chair Warsh strengthened expectations for a rate hike next month. The S&P 500 shed 0.2%, the Nasdaq fell 0.7% and the Dow inched lower. Marvell fell 10.3% despite beating earnings estimates and raising its outlook. Hyperscalers held gains, with Alphabet up 1.5%, Microsoft adding 1.7%, Amazon jumping 4%, and Meta rising 1.2%. Meanwhile, Warsh said underlying inflation was not slowing and pledged to keep fighting price growth until it converges with the Fed’s 2% target during his speech at Jackson Hole Symposium, prompting traders to increase bets on a rate hike next month. Industrial and pharmaceutical stocks declined, with AbbVie losing 1% and Lam Research falling 5.2%.

The CAC 40 rose 1% to close at 8,401 on Friday following the release of domestic and euro area economic data. Euro area consumer confidence edged up to -15.5, its highest level since February, while industrial confidence rose to -5.3, its highest since April 2023. Service sector confidence also improved to 5.8, a seven-month high. Meanwhile, France’s GDP grew 0.7% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026, in line with forecasts. Luxury stocks posted strong gains, with LVMH rising 2.7%, Hermes adding 2.7%, and Kering up 1.4%. Industrial stocks were also mostly higher, with Schneider Electric gaining 1.8% and Air Liquide up 2.1%. EssilorLuxottica rose 3.1% after announcing a share buyback program. Financials advanced despite preliminary data showing French annual inflation accelerating to a three-month high of 2.4% in August, with BNP Paribas up 1.8%, AXA adding 1.8%, and Societe Generale rising 2.1%. Fitch is due to release its assessment of France’s sovereign rating later in the day.

The DAX 40 extended early gains, rising 0.8% to close at a fresh record high of 26,570 on Friday, buoyed by autos stocks, while traders assessed Fed Chair Warsh's speech at the Jackson Hole Economic Symposium. Kevin Warsh struck a hawkish tone, noting that inflation remains elevated and has yet to make enough progress toward the 2% target. His remarks left the door open to tighter policy if inflation does not show clearer signs of easing. Meanwhile, lower oil prices and tech optimism continued to support market sentiment, with traders also monitoring developments in the Middle East. Auto stocks continued their strong upward trend ahead of the weekend. BMW shares led the index, gaining over 4% and benefiting from a more positive outlook from Citigroup. VW and Mercedes Benz Group climbed over 2% each. Utilities, chemical companies, banks, insurers and software firms also posted gains, while chipmaker Infineon Technologies lost ground (-0.6%). The index recorded a weekly rise of 1.7%.

The FTSE 100 rose 0.3% on Friday, joining a broader European rally after Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh struck a cautious tone on inflation at the Jackson Hole symposium. Warsh said recent PCE and CPI readings were encouraging but had not provided enough evidence that underlying inflation was improving decisively, reinforcing expectations that the Fed may need to keep policy restrictive. As a result, market pricing for a September rate hike increased. Financial stocks were among the strongest performers, with HSBC and Standard Chartered both gaining around 1.7%, while Barclays and Lloyds Banking Group also advanced. Utilities also supported the index, led by SSE, which climbed 2.6%, while Severn Trent, Centrica and National Grid posted more modest gains. Shell rose slightly. On the downside, defence stocks BAE Systems and Babcock International declined sharply. Despite Friday’s advance, the FTSE 100 ended August about 0.5% lower, breaking a four-month winning streak.

European stocks closed sharply higher on Friday, recovering from losses in the previous session with support from auto manufacturers, luxury brands, and heavyweight banks. The Euro STOXX 50 gained 1% to 6,492 and the STOXX Europe 600 rose 0.6% to 655, both at their highest in nearly two weeks. Volkswagen, Mercedes-Benz, and BMW jumped between 4% and 3%, ending a volatile week on a positive note as markets continued to assess how the sector is exposed to US tariffs on Mexico and Canada, in addition to benefits from higher industrial profits in China. Strong Chinese economic data also supported luxury brands, with LVMH, Hermes, and Ferrari adding more than 2%. Meanwhile, European banks rose sharply after Fed Chairman Warsh struck a more hawkish tone than previous communication and flattened the global yield curves. BNP Paribas added 2% while UniCredit and BBVA gained 1.5%. Expectations of a rate hike by the ECB were consolidated after the release of higher inflation in France and Spain.
