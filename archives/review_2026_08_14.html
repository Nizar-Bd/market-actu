Persistent geopolitical tensions continued to fuel concerns over supply disruptions and inflation risks, with US President Donald Trump renewing calls for tougher economic pressure on Iran, while Tehran reportedly adopted a more assertive military posture. On the domestic front, France's annual inflation rate rose to 2.1% in July 2026 from 1.8% in June, confirming preliminary estimates and reinforcing expectations that price pressures remain elevated. Among individual stocks, L'Oréal fell 0.2%, Schneider Electric lost 0.5%, Engie dropped 1.0%, and STMicroelectronics declined 1.5%. In contrast, defense and aerospace stocks outperformed, with Airbus rising 1.2%, Thales gaining 1.6%, and Dassault Systèmes climbing 3.9%.

The DAX 40 rose 0.7% to surpass 26,480 on Friday, setting fresh record highs and outperforming its European peers. Efforts to resolve the US-Iran conflict remained stalled amid continued attacks in the region, with Washington also threatening unprecedented economic measures against Tehran next week. The FTSE 100 traded slightly lower on Friday, extending its losses for a fifth consecutive session, as weakness across mining and pharmaceutical stocks weighed on the index. Lower industrial metal prices pressured miners, with Antofagasta falling more than 4%, while Glencore, Endeavour and Fresnillo each declined by more than 2%. Anglo American also dropped around 1.8%. Pharmaceutical stocks were similarly weak, with AstraZeneca and GSK both losing more than 1%. The declines came despite a relatively stronger performance from energy stocks, with Shell and BP trading slightly higher as oil prices remained supported. Aviva also edged up after the insurer reported a 24% increase in first-half profit compared with the previous year. The company said it remained on track to meet its three-year group targets, providing some support to its shares. For the week, the UK index was down around 1.4%.
