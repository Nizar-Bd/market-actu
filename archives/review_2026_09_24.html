US stocks fell further on Thursday as higher Treasury yields reflected an increasingly adverse macroeconomic backdrop. The S&P 500 fell 0.5%, while those for the Nasdaq 100 dropped almost 1% and those for the Dow eased 0.3%. Treasury notes and bonds of all maturities held their plunge from the previous session as PMI data showed an accelerating economy and soaring prices, raising the outlook for interest rates by the Fed. This coincided with dimmed expectations of diplomatic breakthroughs between Iran, the US, and GCC countries, prolonging the blockade on oil exports from the region. The risk of tight financial conditions continued to pressure AI hyperscalers and chip producers as the sector was due to extend its record-setting debt issuance momentum, with Nvidia, Marvell, Intel, Amazon, and Alphabet dropping between 1% and 3%. Meanwhile, Meta dropped 2% to trim this week's rally as markets assessed the prospects of returns on its new AI agent tools.

The CAC 40 fell 0.4% to 8,095 on Thursday, extending losses from the previous session as investors remained cautious amid the lack of tangible progress in US-Iran talks. Tensions remained elevated between the US and Iran as diplomatic talks showed little sign of a breakthrough, driving oil prices higher and keeping bond yields near multi-year highs. Elsewhere, investors monitored the US-China trade truce after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the two economies had agreed to extend the agreement by two months, though Beijing has yet to confirm. Domestically, the consumer confidence index held steady at 86 in September, exceeding the forecast of 85 but remaining well below its long-term average of 100. Meanwhile, the manufacturing business climate indicator was unchanged at 101, matching August's revised reading but falling short of forecasts for 102. Among the biggest decliners were BNP Paribas (-3.6%), STMicroelectronics (-1.3%), and Stellantis (-1.5%).

Frankfurt's DAX 40 fell about 0.2% to around 25,360 on Thursday, extending losses from the prior day, as traders cautiously awaited developments in the Middle East conflict. Geopolitical tensions remained elevated, with diplomatic efforts reportedly showing little sign of progress, supporting oil prices and keeping government bond yields close to multi-year highs. At the same time, concerns over AI risks were mounting among tech leaders after Australia disclosed that an OpenAI agent had breached a government website. Meanwhile, the focus turned to a high-stakes summit between US President Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping set to focus on AI, trade and the Middle East war. The tech sector and autos were the most pressured. Among stocks, BMW (-2.5%), Volkswagen (-2.1%), Rheimental (-2.5%), SAP (-1.6%) and Mercedes-Benz Group (-1.6%) posted the biggest losses. On the flip side, Siemens, Henkel, MTU Aero Engines and Deutche Telekom advanced firmly, each rising more than 1%.

The FTSE 100 traded slightly higher on Thursday, outperforming other European markets, supported by gains in energy and consumer stocks. Shell and BP rose 0.8% and 1.2%, respectively, while Unilever added 0.8% and BAT gained 1.9%. In contrast, pharmaceutical and defence stocks weakened, with AstraZeneca and GSK both down 0.4%, Rolls-Royce falling more than 1% and BAE Systems declining 0.6%. Oil prices remained volatile as the US again indicated that substantial volumes were moving through the Strait of Hormuz, while investors continued to monitor diplomatic efforts to end the Iran war. Among individual stocks, Halma gained 0.2% after raising its margin guidance and maintaining expectations for 30% revenue growth in its AI-exposed Photonics business. Vistry swung to a first-half loss after booking a £475 million impairment and revised its order-book definition. Raspberry Pi said second-half volumes were rising and that memory inventories remained sufficient to meet annual targets.

European stocks traded cautiously on Thursday, with both the STOXX 50 and STOXX 600 swinging around the flatline as investors awaited further developments on the US-Iran conflict. Tensions remained elevated, with little apparent progress towards ending the conflict, pushing oil prices higher once again and keeping government bond yields at multi-year highs. Meanwhile, investors are also looking ahead to today’s summit between Chinese President Xi Jinping and US President Trump, with markets awaiting any concrete developments that could help ease trade tensions, alongside discussions on other key issues. Technology and banking stocks were among the weakest performers. SAP (-1.2%), UBS (-2%), Infineon (-1.6%), Mercedes-Benz (-1.6%) and Rheinmetall (-2.6%) were lower and H&M shares fell nearly 3% after the company’s third-quarter results failed to impress investors. On the other hand, LVMH (0.4%), Novartis (1.1%) and Siemens (0.6%) were higher.
