Frankfurt’s DAX 40 closed marginally down at 25,407 on Monday, as investors weighed the potential for diplomatic progress in the US-Iran conflict, while bracing for key economic data due later this week. Surging oil prices put renewed pressure on sovereign bonds after President Trump rejected Iran’s peace proposal, fueling inflation concerns. Nevertheless, negotiations are set to continue. Meanwhile, ECB President Christine Lagarde, in an address to the European Parliament’s Committee on Economic and Monetary Affairs, defended “moderate” interest rate hikes in response to rising inflation. Rheinmetall led losses, down 1.3%, followed by Volkswagen, Deutsche Telekom, SAP and Hochtief, falling between 0.9%-1.2%. On the upside, Merck climbed 2.6% after signing a deal worth up to $2.13 billion with Shanghai-based SciBrunch Therapeutics for SPR2015, an experimental oral cancer drug targeting the KRAS G12D mutation. Siemens Healthineers, Fresenius Medical Care and Bayer also gained.

The FTSE 100 edged higher on Monday as strong gains in housebuilders and energy stocks were largely offset by broad weakness across the mining sector. Energy companies also advanced, with Shell and BP each rising more than 1% as oil prices strengthened amid the continuing standoff between the US and Iran. Markets remained cautious, however, as hopes for an imminent diplomatic breakthrough in the Middle East conflict faded. Mining stocks came under significant pressure, with Fresnillo falling more than 5% and Endeavour Mining declining over 4% as weaker gold prices weighed on precious-metals producers. Copper prices also fell, adding to pressure across the sector. Antofagasta, Anglo American and Glencore each declined by around 2%, limiting the broader FTSE 100 advance.
