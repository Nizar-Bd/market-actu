The CAC 40 edged lower to around 8,719 on Tuesday, retreating from the record high reached in the previous session as investors grew more cautious amid rising uncertainty surrounding US-Iran relations. Market sentiment weakened after President Donald Trump called on Iran to compensate victims of conflicts involving Tehran, following Iranian demands for reparations over damage caused by recent US and Israeli strikes. The escalating rhetoric reduced expectations for a near-term breakthrough and dimmed hopes for a reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, fueling concerns over continued disruptions to energy supplies and broader risk sentiment. On the corporate front, heavyweight industrial and healthcare stocks came under modest pressure, with Airbus falling 1%, Safran slipping 0.5%, and Sanofi losing 0.3%. Meanwhile, energy shares outperformed, as TotalEnergies gained 1.1%, supported by higher crude prices.

The DAX 40 edged 0.2% lower to below 26,300 on Tuesday morning, retreating slightly from a record high as caution prevailed amid persistent uncertainty over the situation in the Strait of Hormuz. President Donald Trump on Monday responded to Iran’s conditions for a peace deal with demands that Tehran compensate for people killed in wars, attacks and protests, adding to tensions and complicating diplomatic efforts to reopen the crucial waterway. Losses were led by Siemens (-0.5%), Allianz (-0.5%), Airbus (-1.4%), Daimler Truck Holding (-1.4%) and Qiagen (-0.8%). The declines were partly offset by gains in Deutsche Telekom (+0.8%), Siemens Energy (+0.8%), Infineon (+0.5%) and RWE (+0.9%).

The FTSE 100 traded around the flatline on Tuesday after falling 0.4% in the previous session, with gains in energy stocks helping to offset broader weakness. Elsewhere, InterContinental Hotels Group fell more than 2% despite reporting first-half profit above expectations, supported by strong performance in the US and Greater China, which offset disruption in the Middle East. Housebuilder Bellway also warned that the near-term outlook remained uncertain despite exceeding forecasts for home completions and housing revenue. On the data front, the BRC reported that UK retail sales rose 1.3% year-on-year in July, below the 12-month average of 1.8%. However, Barclays’ broader consumer spending measure increased 2%, marking its strongest growth of the year.

European stocks pared their early gains on Tuesday, with both the STOXX 50 and STOXX 600 hovering around the flatline near record levels.
