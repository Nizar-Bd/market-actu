The CAC 40 fell 0.3% to 8,692.7 on Wednesday, extending losses from the previous session as investors remained cautious amid mounting uncertainty over the swift reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Concerns about supply disruptions lingered despite comments from Pakistan’s defense minister that the US and Iran were “close to some sort of arrangement” regarding the strategic waterway, while reports also pointed to meaningful progress in talks between Tehran and Oman. Luxury stocks weighed heavily on the index, with LVMH falling 1.8%, Hermès losing 1.6%, and Kering dropping 2.4%. The sector remained under pressure as a prolonged slowdown in global luxury demand complicated succession planning at Armani Group, where the proposed sale of an initial 15% stake could reportedly be pushed back beyond March 2027. In contrast, STMicroelectronics added 0.6%, benefiting from strength across global tech markets.

The DAX 40 edged up to around 26,430 on Wednesday, marking a fresh high and extending its winning run to five sessions. Gains were largely driven by technology and industrial stocks, outweighing losses in consumer cyclicals, telecommunications companies, and automakers. TKMS jumped over 11% as investors welcomed solid results and an upgraded full-year revenue outlook from the naval shipbuilder. On the downside, TUI dropped nearly 2% after Europe's largest travel company missed Q3 operating profit expectations. Brenntag fell 1.6% despite reporting solid second-quarter performance, driven by higher chemical prices, and raised its full-year earnings outlook.
