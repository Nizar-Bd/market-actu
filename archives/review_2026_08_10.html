The CAC 40 traded flat around 8,714 on Monday, hovering near record highs as investors turned cautious amid ongoing uncertainty over the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. US President Donald Trump said Washington was only "semi-negotiating" with Tehran and would wait for economic pressure on Iran to intensify. Meanwhile, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi denied any direct talks with the US and reiterated that the Strait would remain closed until Tehran's demands, including the lifting of the naval blockade are met. The impasse kept concerns over energy supplies, inflationary pressures, and the interest-rate outlook firmly in focus. Luxury stocks outperformed, with LVMH (+1.3%), Hermès (+0.6%), and Kering (+0.5%) leading gains. On the downside, Airbus (-0.4%), Safran (-0.5%), and BNP Paribas (-0.8%) were among the decliners.

European stocks opened the second week of August on a mixed-to-positive note, with the STOXX 50 edging 0.2% higher to set a new record, while the STOXX 600 traded around the flatline, remaining close to the record levels reached last week. The indices gained 2.6% and 1.7%, respectively, in the previous week. Investor sentiment remained cautious amid ongoing uncertainty in the Middle East, with markets awaiting further clarity on the prospects of a deal between the US and Iran. The technology sector was among the top performers, with ASML Holding rising 1.5% and Infineon Technologies gaining 2.1%. Luxury stocks also advanced, with LVMH up 1% and Hermes adding 0.4%. On the downside, Vistry plunged more than 8% to the bottom of the STOXX 600 after the FT reported that Allianz Trade is reducing the credit cover it provides to Vistry’s suppliers. The move could further intensify pressure on the cash flow of the struggling UK housebuilder.
