Tech shares plunged on Thursday after the Financial Times reported that OpenAI's revenue was sharply below figures published by outlets previously. The S&P 500 fell 0.8% and the Nasdaq 100 slipped 1.6%, while the Dow held slightly below the flatline. The report cited documents indicating annualized revenue of $50 billion rather than the $70 billion previously estimated. This reignited concerns over profitability for LLM developers and the sustainability of their soaring investments in AI infrastructure, which have carried both the stock market and US GDP growth this year. Nvidia fell 3%, while Broadcom, SpaceX, AMD, Intel, and Arm dropped between 4% and 8%. Meanwhile, energy prices eased after President Trump signaled the US would not strike Iran before the midterm elections, cooling the rally in long-dated yields. Consumer defensive shares and payment processors rebounded in relief, with Walmart, Visa, and Mastercard adding more than 2%.

The CAC 40 fell 0.5% to close at 7,730 on Thursday, extending losses and hitting a seven-month low amid strains in global bond markets and a surge in oil prices. Oil prices jumped on reports that the US is planning new strikes against Iran, risking the rebound in Middle Eastern oil exports and intensifying inflationary pressures. Global yields also extended their rally as a hawkish US Federal Reserve backdrop pressured credit-sensitive stocks. Bank of France Governor and ECB Governing Council member Emmanuel Moulin warned that the Middle East crisis is affecting global economies through inflation and bond markets. His comments came amid France’s fiscal challenges. Losses were broad-based, with financials and industrial stocks posting sharper losses. BNP paribas fell 2.4% and Safran lost 2.7%. Luxury stocks also posted losses, with LVMH down 1.9%. In contrast, TotalEnergies rose 2.9%.

Frankfurt's DAX closed 1.2% lower at 24,823 on Thursday, reaching it lowest level in almost two months, amid elevated energy prices and persistent concerns over Europe's fiscal outlook. High sovereign yields due to French concerns continue to pressure banks and financials. Deutsch Bank fell 0.7%, while Commerzbank declined 2.4% as Germany’s finance minister said he was coordinating with counterparts on UniCredit’s takeover bid for the German lender. Tech and industrials were also under pressure, with Infineon down 3.4% and Siemens falling 2.6%. Automakers fell as European buyers continue to shift toward electric vehicles amid higher fuel prices, with Volkswagen down 4.5%. BMW (2.8%) and Mercedes Benz (2%) also fell. On the upside, defense Rheinmetall (0.9%) advanced while British Premier Burnham stated the UK would be open to Germany joining a partnershipto develop a new fighter jet alongside other countries. The FTSE 100 closed slightly lower at 10,441 on Thursday, holding the sharp decline from the previous session amid further pressure from the financial sector. Gilt yields surged to multi-decade highs in the longer end of the curve as higher energy prices exacerbated inflationary risks from fuel and power prices. Banks remained burdened by higher lending costs, with HSB, Lloyds, Barclays, NatWest, and Standard Chartered losing around 2%. Pharmaceutical giants also underwent pressure in the session with GSK sinking 3.1% and AstraZeneca losing 2.3%. On the other hand, Tesco gained 5.2% after first-half adjusted operating profit rose 6.3% to £1.78 billion, prompting the supermarket to raise its full-year guidance and increase its share buyback to £950 million.

European stocks closed sharply lower for a second session on Thursday as sovereign yields reignited their surge. The Euro STOXX 50 fell 0.8% to 6,132 and the STOXX Europe 600 dropped 0.7% to 625.7. Benchmark bond yields across the eurozone's largest economies extended yesterday's surge, especially among more indebted member states, reinforcing concerns over fragmentation across eurozone sovereign bond markets. Contagion fears continued to weigh on banking stocks, with UniCredit, Intesa Sanpaolo, ING, and Santander dropping between 2.5% and 3.1%. In addition, accounts of the latest ECB meeting confirmed that the Governing Council continues to see upside inflation risks in the bloc's economy. Meanwhile, Argenx tumbled over 15% after discontinuing its late-stage study of Vyvgart, a drug for adults with Sjögren's disease.
