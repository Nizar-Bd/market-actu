US equities were lower on Tuesday as higher energy prices dented the outlook on margins and lifted risks of higher interest rates. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 were 0.4% down, and the Dow fell nearly 1%. New strikes between the US and Iran prolonged expectations on suspended fuel exports from the Persian Gulf, while attacks from Houthi forces damaged Saudi energy infrastructure and dented their capacity. Borrowing costs for US companies were also lifted by hawkish signs from the Bank of Japan and likely selling of US Treasury securities by Japan to support the yen. Credit-sensitive hyperscalers were firmly lower, with Microsoft and Alphabet down 1%, while Amazon fell 1% ahead of its sterling bond offer. AI companies have increasingly borrowed in foreign currencies as dollar bond markets already face soaring AI-debt supply. In turn, Oracle gained 5% ahead of its earnings this week.

The CAC 40 fell 0.4% to 8,270 on Tuesday, reversing the previous session’s gains and hitting its lowest level since July, as investors weighed rising oil prices and inflation pressures amid escalating Middle East tensions. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said Iran and Oman were in the final stages of an agreement to establish a safe maritime route through the Strait of Hormuz, though disruptions to oil shipments continued to fuel supply concerns. The European Central Bank now expects to raise borrowing costs by 25 bps, especially after eurozone inflation accelerated to 3.3% in August, its highest level in nearly three years and well above the ECB’s 2% target. Banking stocks came under pressure, particularly BNP Paribas (-1%), Crédit Agricole (-0.8%) and Société Générale (-2.4%). Other notable laggards included Hermès (-1.3%), STMicroelectronics (-1.6%) and Airbus (-0.9%).

The DAX 40 was down for a second session on Tuesday, falling 0.3% to around 25,930, in line with its European peers. Persistently high oil and gas prices amid continued hostilities in the Middle East fueled inflation worries ahead of this week’s European Central Bank meeting. The ECB is expected to raise borrowing costs by another 25 basis points to 2.5% on Thursday, which would mark the second rate hike this year. The technology sector was the hardest hit, alongside banks, insurers and industrials. Infineon Technologies slumped nearly 3%, making it the worst performer, following a downgrade by Morgan Stanley to "equal weight" from "overweight". Other notable decliners indluded Heidelberg Materials, Continental, Siemens Energy, Adidas, Airbus and Deutsche Bank, with losses ranging between 1%-2%. On the upside, Fresenius SE & Co and Scout24 advanced the most, rising 1.7% and 1.5%, respectively.

The FTSE 100 traded slightly lower as global stocks weakened and Brent crude approached $100 a barrel, reinforcing expectations that central banks may need to keep raising interest rates to contain inflation. Banks were among the worst performers, with Lloyds Banking Group, HSBC Holdings and Barclays falling between 1.3% and 1.7%, while NatWest and Standard Chartered also declined. Pharma stocks AstraZeneca and GSK slipped around 0.5%-0.7%, and Rolls-Royce was down 0.9%. In contrast, Computacenter jumped 4% after strong results, benefiting from the boom in data-centre spending. Rising oil prices supported energy stocks, with BP up 1.5% and Shell gaining 1%. Miners also advanced as copper prices reached another record high, lifting Antofagasta 1.7% and Glencore 1.3%. Meanwhile, UK retail sales grew 0.7% in August, according to the British Retail Consortium, below the 1.6% 12-month average and 3.1% growth recorded in August last year.

European stocks fell on Tuesday, with both the STOXX 50 and STOXX 600 down 0.3% following a subdued session the previous day. Oil prices extended their gains as tensions in the Middle East remained elevated. Meanwhile, natural gas prices hovered near their highest levels in more than three years, adding to inflationary pressures and strengthening expectations that further monetary-policy tightening may be needed. The ECB is expected to raise borrowing costs by another 25 basis points this week. Shares of Novartis tumbled almost 9%, making it the worst performer on the STOXX 600, after its pelacarsen drug failed to reduce cardiovascular events despite lowering harmful cholesterol. The results raise questions about one of the pharmaceutical industry’s most closely watched drug races. Infineon also fell 2.6%, while ASML slipped 0.7% despite securing commitments from top chipmakers, including Samsung and TSMC, to use its latest semiconductor production equipment.
