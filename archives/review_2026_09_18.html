US stock indices were muted on Friday, loosely holding yesterday's rebound as energy prices held their mid-week retreated and paused the momentum of a deteriorating macroeconomic backdrop. The CAC 40 fell 0.6% on Friday, retreating after two consecutive sessions of gains and heading for its sixth straight weekly decline as investors weighed the latest decisions from major central banks. Notably, the Federal Reserve and the Bank of Japan raised rates earlier this week, while the Bank of England left interest rates unchanged. Meanwhile, traders pared back bets on further ECB tightening despite the Federal Reserve’s more hawkish stance, citing concerns that persistently high energy prices could weigh on economic growth and eventually temper inflationary pressures. Domestically, Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu is working to hammer out a deficit-reduction budget for 2027 as growing bond-market pressure and a rising cost of living heighten fiscal strains. Among the notable laggards, LVMH fell 0.8%, TotalEnergies declined 1.1%, BNP Paribas dropped 1%, AXA lost 2.5%, and Essilor slipped 1.1%. In contrast, STMicroelectronics climbed 2.1%.

Frankfurt’s DAX 40 fell more than 1.5% to around 25,300 on Friday afternoon, its lowest since late July, as caution returned to markets on the so-called “quadruple-witching” day. Meanwhile, investors focused on developments in the Middle East ahead of next week’s summit between US President Trump and Gulf-state leaders. Domestically, attention is turning to state elections in Berlin and Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania on Sept. 20, with the results set to provide another test of Chancellor Friedrich Merz’s leadership after the AfD’s strong showing in Saxony-Anhalt. Most sectors came under pressure, particularly telecoms, autos, banks and insurers, while technology outperformed. Deutsche Telekom led losses, slipping 4.7%, following a negative analyst report for its French competitor Orange. Volkswagen, BMW, Mercedes-Benz and Deutsche Bank were also among the worst performers. On the upside, Infineon Technologies stood out, rising 3%. For the week, the index was set to drop about 1.1%.

London’s FTSE 100 fell 0.3% to 10,785 points on Friday, retreating after two consecutive sessions of gains as investors weighed recent decisions from major central banks and falling oil prices. The Bank of England held rates steady on Thursday but warned that a prolonged Middle East conflict could prompt tighter policy, while the Federal Reserve and Bank of Japan raised rates this week and signaled scope for further tightening this year. Meanwhile, oil prices extended their decline for a third session as Saudi Arabia worked to restore flows through its East-West pipeline, while US President Donald Trump is due to meet Gulf leaders next week. In domestic data, UK retail sales rose 0.5% in August, defying expectations for a 0.2% decline and rebounding from July’s fall, with department stores particularly strong as retailers resolved stock availability issues.

European stocks were slightly lower on Friday, with both the STOXX 50 and the STOXX 600 falling 0.1%, pausing after gains in the previous two sessions, as traders ended a week packed with monetary policy decisions on a cautious note. The Bank of Japan raised interest rates to a 31-year high, as expected. However, two policymakers dissented and voted to keep rates unchanged, raising concerns among investors about whether the central bank will be able to sustain its tightening cycle. Meanwhile, oil prices extended their decline. Telecom, energy, insurance and auto shares were in the red, while technology stocks outperformed. Shell fell 1.2%, Nestle declined 1%, and Allianz lost 1.3%. Orange tumbled nearly 4%, making it the worst performer on the STOXX 600. On the other hand, ASML Holding gained 2.1% and STMicroelectronics rose 2.2%, ranking among the index's top performers. On the week, the STOXX 50 is little changed while the STOXX 600 is up 0.5%.
