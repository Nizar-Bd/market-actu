The CAC 40 rose 0.9% to 8,139 on Monday, recovering from losses the previous week as the continued decline in oil prices eased concerns over inflationary pressures. Energy-driven inflation fears eased amid renewed efforts to reduce tensions in the Middle East and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, after Tehran conveyed conditions to the US through diplomatic mediators for ending the war. Global bond yields moved lower. Banking stocks posted gains, with Societe Generale up 1.8%. Luxury stocks also advanced amid lower risk aversion, with L’Oreal rising 2.1% and Hermes up 0.9%. Industrial stocks were mostly higher, with Safran rising 2.4% and Air Liquide up 1.3%. Schneider Electric jumped 3.2% and STMicroelectronics added 2.7% as US chipmakers posted sharp gains. In contrast, TotalEnergies lost 1% on the oil pullback.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 extended early gains to close about 1.1% firmer at 25,575 on Monday, supported by lower oil prices and easing bond yields amid hopes of a diplomatic resolution to the Middle East conflict. Sentiment was also lifted by constructive US-China talks ahead of this week’s Trump-Xi summit. The discussions are expected to include AI, which boosted technology stocks such as Infineon Technologies (+4.2%). Other top gainers included Siemens (+3.5%), MTU Aero Engines (+3%), Commerzbank (+2.8%) and Deutsche Post (+2.5%). On the downside, Volkswagen fell 1.8% after being removed from the EURO STOXX 50 for the first time in 15 years, while a profit warning added to the pressure. Meanwhile, traders digested the results of elections in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and Berlin, where parties on the right and left gained ground, raising pressure on Chancellor Friedrich Merz and his reform plans.

The FTSE 100 rose 0.8% on Monday, recovering from a 1.5% decline on Friday as falling oil prices eased concerns over persistent inflation and improved global risk appetite. Oil extended its losses into a fourth session on hopes of diplomatic progress in the Middle East ahead of this week’s UN meeting. Banks led the gains, with HSBC up 1.7%, Barclays 1.8%, Lloyds Banking Group 1.1%, NatWest 1.5% and Standard Chartered 1.8%. Mining stocks also advanced as copper prices gained on expectations of tighter supply and signs of resilient Chinese demand, with Antofagasta rising 3.5% and Anglo American 2.6%. JD Sports climbed 0.8% after announcing a long-term franchise partnership with Mexico’s Grupo Axo, marking its first entry into the Mexican market. In contrast, oil majors declined as crude prices fell to their lowest level in recent weeks, with BP down 3% and Shell losing 1.3%.

European stocks closed firmly higher on Monday with support from lower sovereign yields, easing ongoing pressures from an adverse macroeconomic backdrop. The Euro STOXX 50 gained 1.3% to 6,318 and the STOXX Europe 600 rose 1.0% to 642. Natural gas and fuel prices pulled back following the weekend amid signs that GCC nations are chasing diplomatic ways with Iran to restart tanker flows through the Persian Gulf. The drop in yields aided the outlook of credit and supported banks, with UniCredit, Nordea, and BBVA adding between 3.2% and 2%. Lower gas prices also supported power-heavy industrial giants, with Siemens and Schneider each adding more than 3%. Lastly, a global rally for the AI infrastructure sector supported Infineon with a 4.3% jump, while ASML added 2.5%. Nokia and Engie both gained as they were added to the Euro STOXX 50, replacing Wolters Kluwer and Volkswagen.
