US stocks were mostly higher on Thursday, with traditional economic sectors offsetting weakness for chip producers after Treasury yields halted their surge from recent weeks. AI infrastructure stocks tracked the losses, with Sandisk, and Marvell down more than 1%. Meanwhile, Hewlett Packard Enterprises fell 4.3% after its results fell short of expectations.

The CAC 40 fell 0.2% to 8,263 on Thursday, extending losses for a fourth consecutive session and slipping to its lowest level since mid-June, led by weakness in the luxury sector. LVMH dropped 1.9%, while Hermès and Kering both shed 2.4%, as the sector remained under pressure despite early signs of stabilizing consumer sentiment. Investors remained cautious as stimulus measures in China, the industry's most important growth market, have yet to translate into a meaningful recovery in demand, leaving luxury groups struggling to regain momentum after the disruption caused by the Iran conflict. In contrast, Legrand rose 2.6% after Deutsche Bank upgraded the stock to "Buy" from "Hold." Further weighing on sentiment, final data showed the Composite PMI falling to 48.5 in August from 49.4 in July, as a sharper contraction in services (48 vs 49.6) more than offset an expansion in manufacturing (51.1 vs 49.8).

The DAX 40 edged higher to around 25,900 on Thursday, though remaining near one-month lows, reflecting a cautious sentiment across markets. Elevated Middle East hostilities continued to worry investors, though President Trump’s remarks that the conflict would not last “too long” provided some reassurance. Oil prices and bond yields also stabilised after their sharp rise earlier in the week, helping to support broader sentiment. Telecommunications, software and chemical stocks led sectoral gains, while industrials, travel-related stocks and autos came under pressure. Deutche Telekom was among the top performers, rising 1.4%, following reports that Elliott investment built up a larger stake in an attempt to prevent a multi-billion dollar merger with T-Mobile US. SAP, Siemens Energy and Heidelberg Materials also advanced firmly, each adding over 1%, while Rheinmetall (-1.9%) and Airbus (-1.7%) lagged.

London’s FTSE 100 steadied around 10,750 points on Thursday, its lowest level in two weeks, after two sessions of losses as the global bond selloff eased. Oil prices edged lower after President Donald Trump said renewed US attacks on Iran were likely to be short-lived, helping ease concerns over inflation and further energy-price pressures. Metals prices rebounded as fears of a wider Middle East escalation eased, potentially reducing pressure on central banks to raise interest rates and supporting demand for non-interest-bearing precious metals. BP and Shell fell around 0.7%, while miners Fresnillo, Endeavour, Glencore, Anglo American, Rio Tinto and Antofagasta gained 0.5%-1%. M&G dropped 2.2%, Compass 1.4% and NEXT 1%, while Airtel Africa, Vodafone and London Stock Exchange rose 3.2%, 2.4% and 1.1%, respectively.

European stocks traded cautiously on Thursday, with both the STOXX 50 and STOXX 600 fluctuating around the flatline at one-month lows as investors continued to assess the situation in the Middle East, where hostilities remain elevated.
