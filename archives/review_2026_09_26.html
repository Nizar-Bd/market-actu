The CAC 40 closed little changed at 8,078 on Friday as lower oil prices and calmer bond markets supported equities, while losses in the luxury sector weighed on the index. Reports that the US and Iran were exploring a agreement that could lead to the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz eased concerns over energy supply disruptions. The pullback in oil offered respite to credit-sensitive sectors that have been under pressure from energy-driven inflation concerns. Financial stocks rose, with BNP Paribas adding 0.9%, Crédit Agricole rising 1.4%, and Société Générale advancing 3.5%. Meanwhile, luxury stocks closed lower amid renewed pressure from weaker Chinese demand, subdued consumer confidence, and softer earnings expectations. Hermès shed 0.5%, while Kering lost 1.1%. Industrials closed mixed, with Airbus (-1%) and Thales (-1.2%) down, while Schneider Electric (+0.4%) and STMicroelectronics (+0.5%) gained, tracking gains from US chipmakers. On the week, the CAC 40 shed 0.7%.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 closed about 0.5% firmer at 25,396 on Friday, halting a two-day losing streak and booking a 0.4% weekly gain. Optimism surrounding a potential peace agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz pushed oil prices lower, although uncertainties persisted. Meanwhile, the US-China talks secured a two-month extension of the fragile trade truce ahead of its November expiry, marking the biggest achievement so far. However, little progress was made on other key issues, including AI, Iran and Taiwan. On the domestic data front, German consumer sentiment weakened more than expected heading into October. Most sectors recorded gains, led by banks, travel-related srocks, tech and industrials. Adidas led gains among stocks, surging about 3%, after Metzler Capital Markets reaffirmed its 'buy' rating. Bank stocks were also in strong demand, with Commerzbank and Deutsche Bank rising around 2.7% each. Conversely, BASF fell 3.6% after Evonik confirmed talks on a potential takeover.

The FTSE 100 edged higher on Friday as gains in banking and mining stocks offset weakness among energy companies. HSBC Holdings, Barclays and Lloyds advanced between 0.8% and 1.8%, while Glencore rose 1.6% and Anglo American gained 1.8%. United Utilities also moved slightly higher after the water company said its operational performance remained in line with expectations and that it was seeking support for £1 billion of investment in its network. Oil majors underperformed, with BP falling more than 2% and Shell declining 0.8% as crude prices weakened. Oil markets continued to weigh the possibility of a US-Iran truce against concerns that escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia could disrupt supplies from the key Middle Eastern producer. Meanwhile, UK consumer confidence improved for a third consecutive month, according to GfK, although the survey pointed to continued caution among households. The FTSE 100 was around 0.3% higher over the week.

European stocks closed higher on Friday as energy prices moderated and softened the recent surge in inflationary risks. The Euro STOXX 50 added 0.4% to 6,296 and the STOXX Europe 600 gained 0.3% to 638.5. Sovereign yields held off from extending their surges from the week as a pullback in natural gas prices limited risks of worsening inflationary pressures at the turn of the fourth quarter. Banks rebounded after underperforming in the last sessions, with UniCredit, Deutsche Bank, and ING gaining between 3% and 1%. To add, stocks with exposure to AI software and infrastructure also rebounded, tracking the long momentum in the US with ASML, Infineon, and Prosus jumping more than 1.5%. On the economic data front, German consumer sentiment deteriorated more sharply than expected heading into October, as higher energy prices weighed on households’ income expectations.
