The CAC 40 rose 0.3% to 8,591 on Thursday, marking its third consecutive session of gains and setting a fresh record high, as market sentiment remained upbeat amid a new wave of corporate earnings. Notably, Schneider Electric climbed 2.6% after reporting revenue that exceeded market expectations for 2025. In addition, Engie surged 6.8%, reaching its highest level since February 2011, after posting robust earnings, strong cash flow, and solid growth in renewables, alongside the announcement of its acquisition of UK Power Networks (UKPN). In contrast, AXA slipped 0.2%, despite delivering record growth, profitability, and capital strength, and outlining a confident outlook for 2026. Other financial stocks also declined, with BNP Paribas, Crédit Agricole, and Société Générale falling between 0.1% and 0.9%. On the domestic front, investors are awaiting France’s final Q4 GDP figures, along with the latest inflation data due tomorrow.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 edged down to around 25,100 on Thursday, following slight gains in the prior session, as investors digested a flurry of corporate earnings, including results from chip giant Nvidia. Meanwhile, global attention turned to another round of US-Iran nuclear talks scheduled to take place today in Geneva, aimed at averting a potential conflict. Insurers were among the top losers after Allianz reported record 2025 operating profit but issued 2026 guidance below analysts’ expectations. Deutsche Telekom rose just 0.2% as it posted better-than-expected Q4 core profits, yet offered mixed guidance for 2026. Outside the main index, Puma shares jumped 8% after the sportswear giant posted a narrower-than-expected loss in 2025. The company said sales are expected to keep falling this year, albeit at a slower pace in the low- to mid-single-digit range.

European stocks were little changed near record highs on Thursday as investors digested a fresh wave of corporate earnings. The Euro Stoxx 50 added 0.2% to 6,185, while the broader STOXX Europe 600 was flat at 633. Among notable movers, Engie surged 7% after announcing plans to acquire 100% of UK Power Networks, and LSEG climbed 5.9% on an additional £3 billion share buyback amid pressure from Elliott Investment Management. Rolls-Royce jumped 6% after raising its profit outlook and announcing up to a $12 billion share buyback, while Puma gained 4% on stronger-than-expected results, despite canceling its dividend and forecasting another loss this year. Schneider Electric, Eni, Deutsche Telekom, and Clariant also posted gains after upbeat results. In contrast, Stellantis fell 1% after reporting its first-ever annual loss, driven by €25.4 billion in write-downs as it scaled back its electric vehicle strategy, and Allianz slipped 0.9% after issuing 2026 guidance below forecasts.
