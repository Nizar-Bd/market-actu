The CAC 40 edged up 0.1% to around 8,347 on Tuesday, following a steady performance in the previous session as hopes for a temporary de-escalation of Middle East tensions supported sentiment after reports emerged that Iran had received a 10-day ceasefire proposal from mediators. The development, aimed at reviving the interim agreement reached last month, offered some relief despite escalating regional tensions, with the US carrying out a tenth consecutive day of strikes against Iran and Tehran continuing retaliatory attacks across the region. Meanwhile, attention also remained focused on the corporate earnings season. STMicroelectronics advanced 2.4% ahead of its second-quarter 2026 results due later this week, after reporting an earnings miss in the previous quarter. Other notable gainers included TotalEnergies (1.5%), Schneider Electric (1.3%), and Airbus (0.5%). In contrast, luxury giant LVMH fell 0.5%, while EssilorLuxottica and Saint-Gobain lost 1.3% and 1.0%, respectively.

The FTSE 100 fell on Tuesday after losing 0.7% in the previous session, as investors assessed the appointment of John Healey as chancellor, fresh UK economic data, and lower oil prices. Healey, the former defence secretary, was named by Prime Minister Andy Burnham after Monday's close. Burnham has pledged to cut household energy bills by scrapping the Digital ID programme. Meanwhile, June public borrowing came in lower than expected, while labour market data was broadly in line with forecasts, with wage growth easing slightly in the three months to May. Shell and BP declined as Brent crude slipped below $89 per barrel. AstraZeneca and GSK fell 0.6% to 0.8%, while Unilever, BAT, and Polar Capital also traded lower. Miners outperformed, led by Antofagasta, Glencore, Anglo American, Rio Tinto, Fresnillo, and Endeavour. Defence stocks also advanced, with Babcock, BAE Systems, and Rolls-Royce posting solid gains.
