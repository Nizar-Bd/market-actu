US stocks rose Wednesday, with S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 futures up 0.4% and Dow futures gaining 100 points, as softer-than-expected inflation data boosted hopes that the Federal Reserve may hold off on another rate hike as soon as next month. The PCE price index rose 0.3% in August, below expectations of 0.4%, while core PCE increased 0.2%, also below forecasts of 0.3%. Year-on-year, headline PCE inflation stood at 3.4%, below expectations of 3.7%. Markets are now pricing in a below 40% chance of a 25 bps rate hike in October. Most megacap and growth stocks edged higher, with Alphabet and Apple gaining slightly and Microsoft up 1%. Chip stocks also advanced broadly, with Intel jumping more than 3%. Despite the gains, market performance in September was mixed amid high bond-market volatility, elevated oil prices linked to the US-Iran war, and expectations of further policy tightening later this year. Still, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq are on track for a second consecutive quarterly gain.

The CAC 40 fell 0.9% to close at 7,964 on Wednesday amid a lack of progress in US-Iran negotiations. Oil and fuel prices rose amid concerns over energy-driven inflation, pressuring credit-sensitive stocks as expectations for more hawkish central-bank increased. France’s preliminary CPI data showed annual inflation up to 3% in September, its highest level since February 2024. Financials fell, with BNP Paribas down 2.5% and AXA losing 2.2%. Industrials were mostly lower, with Schneider Electric falling 1.5%, Thales down 0.9%, and Vinci shedding 1.4%. The pharmaceutical and chemical subsectors gained, with Sanofi up 1.6%, EssilorLuxottica rising 0.7%, and Air Liquide adding 1.2%. Meanwhile, household consumption of goods in France fell 0.5% in August, reversing a 0.4% increase in July and marking the first monthly decline since April. Luxury stocks posted losses, with LVMH and Hermes both down 1.2%. TotalEnergies lost 3.1% as investors took profits. On the month, the CAC 40 lost 5.2%.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 lost further ground to close about 0.8% down at 25,211 on Wednesday, hitting the lowest since July 24, mirroring its European peers. Market sentiment remained pressured by surging oil prices and elevated bond yields amid persistent Middle East risks. Meanwhile, investors assessed a batch of economic data from Europe and the US, particularly focused on inflation. Signs of rising price pressures across major eurozone economies raised expectations that the ECB may need to keep monetary policy tight for longer. Media, insurance, industrials, telecom and banks were the hardest hit, while retailers and tech advanced the most. Commerzbank shares slipped 4.9%, leading losses following a Deutsche Bank downgrade to Hold from Buy and growing expectations of a UniCredit takeover. The index recorded a monthly decline of roughly 4%, but posted a 0.9% quarterly gain.

The FTSE 100 fell for a third consecutive session on Wednesday, reaching a three-week low and ending September 2% lower. The decline followed a 0.4% monthly drop in August, marking two consecutive months of losses, although the index still gained 1.1% over the third quarter. That quarterly advance was supported by a 5.3% rise in July and extended the FTSE 100’s winning streak to seven quarters, its longest in more than two decades. Banks, energy and pharmaceutical stocks weighed on Wednesday’s performance. HSBC declined 0.7%, while Barclays and Lloyds Banking Group lost 0.4% and Standard Chartered fell 1%. Shell slipped 0.6%, with BP also slightly lower, while AstraZeneca and GSK dropped 0.7% and 1.3%, respectively. Antofagasta gained 2.7%, while SSE and National Grid rose 2% and 1.8%. Meanwhile, revised data showed UK GDP expanded 0.5% in Q2, up from the previous estimate of 0.4%.

European equities ended September under pressure on Wednesday, as elevated global bond yields and persistent inflation concerns weighed on investor sentiment ahead of the final quarter. The STOXX 50 declined 0.8%, while the broader STOXX 600 fell 0.4%, with most sectors ending in negative territory. Banks weakened by around 0.5%, while energy and food & beverage stocks were among the biggest decliners, each losing 1.4%. Technology stocks bucked the broader trend, rising 2.5% to reach their highest level in a month and leading sector gains. The monthly performance was weaker overall, with both the STOXX 50 and STOXX 600 down more than 2% in September. For the STOXX 600, the decline ended a five-month run of monthly gains. Over the third quarter, both benchmarks fell 1%, marking a challenging period for European equities.
