US stocks rose on Monday with support from a rebound in Treasury securities. The S&P 500 rose 0.5%, the Dow added over 600 points, and the Nasdaq inched higher. The US refrained from striking Iran over the weekend and fuel prices pulled back. The moves drove yields to decline across the curve as markets readjusted inflationary risks, supporting credit-sensitive equity sectors. Bank holding companies were higher, with JPMorgan, Bank of America, Visa, and Goldman Sachs gaining over 1%. Large-cap tech companies also gained, with Amazon soaring 5%. Meanwhile, Bristol-Myers Squibb surged nearly 10% amid reports that it could be acquired by AstraZeneca. In turn, chip producers extended their volatile momentum as markets consistently reconsider their speculative exposure and overleveraged investors liquidate positions at likely discounts. Micron and Sandisk dropped over 4%, setting the pace for memory chip producers. Meanwhile, SpaceX and AMD were 1% down ahead of their earnings tomorrow.

The CAC 40 climbed 1% to 8,591 on the first trading day of August, reaching its highest level since late February, as investors embraced renewed hopes for diplomacy after US President Donald Trump announced that talks with Iran would resume following his decision to halt a planned strike. The easing geopolitical tensions drove oil prices lower and encouraged markets to scale back bets on additional ECB tightening, although a September hike remains widely expected. Market optimism was further supported by a fresh batch of corporate earnings reports and company updates. Luxury stocks advanced, with LVMH (1.4%), Hermès (1.2%) and Kering (0.2%) gaining after Kering reported 1% first-half revenue growth as Gucci’s decline narrowed. Industrial heavyweights also supported the index, including Schneider Electric (1.4%), Airbus (3%) and Safran (2.8%), following Airbus’ strong first-half performance and progress toward resolving its Pratt & Whitney dispute.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 rose more than 1% to a record high of 25,990 points in the first trading session of August, as a sharp drop in oil prices lifted risk appetite by easing concerns over inflation and higher interest rates. Crude prices fell on hopes of renewed US-Iran talks after President Donald Trump said negotiations would begin on Monday, though he gave no deadline for a deal. Markets were also supported by mostly upbeat corporate earnings and a continued recovery in global AI-related stocks. Corporate news was limited ahead of a busier earnings calendar later this week. Stabilus gained more than 1% after its detailed quarterly results confirmed preliminary figures, while E.ON rose 1.5% after Jefferies upgraded the stock to "Buy" from "Hold" and raised its price target to €22.70 from €17.40.

The FTSE 100 fluctuated on Monday, with gains capped by weakness in energy stocks and AstraZeneca. Optimism surrounding a potential agreement that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz pushed crude oil prices lower, weighing on oil majors Shell and BP, which both fell more than 2%. AstraZeneca dropped over 6% after reports that the pharmaceutical group had held discussions with US drugmaker Bristol Myers Squibb about a possible merger. In contrast, homebuilders Persimmon and Barratt Redrow gained more than 4% as markets scaled back expectations for further Bank of England rate hikes. Banks also advanced, while aerospace engineer Rolls-Royce rose around 3.8%.

European stocks started August on a positive note, with the STOXX 50 rising 0.9% and the STOXX 600 gaining 0.3%, as investors prepared for a fresh round of corporate earnings and welcomed lower oil prices after US President Donald Trump said new US-Iran talks would begin on Monday. Airlines led the gains, with Lufthansa (+3.8%) and Ryanair (+3.3%) among the top performers. On the downside, AstraZeneca tumbled more than 6% following reports that it was in merger talks with Bristol Myers Squibb on a potential $400 billion deal. Energy stocks also came under pressure as oil prices declined, with Eni (-2.2%), TotalEnergies (-1.8%), and Shell (-1.1%) all trading lower.
