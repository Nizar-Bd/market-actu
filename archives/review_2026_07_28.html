Futures tracking US equity indices were mixed on Tuesday as chip producers extended their selloff, contrasting with relief on other sectors from lower Treasury yields. Contracts for the S&P 500 were flat, while those for the Nasdaq 100 dropped 1% and those for the Dow gained 350 points. Concerns that spending on AI infrastructure by hyperscalers is due to slow grappled compute and memory producers, with Micron, Sandisk dropping more than 7%. The sector was also punished by optimism on Chinese competitors following the strong CXMT IPO in Shanghai. Nvidia fell 1.5% and AMD lost around 5%. On the other hand, software-facing and non-tech shares were mostly higher. Credit costs extended their pullback, tracking lower energy pries as US officials signaled willingness for diplomacy with Iran. JPMorgan was higher to set the pace for the banks, while Visa gained 1% ahead of its earning release after the close. Also, Coca Cola gained 3% premarket after boosting its outlook.

The CAC 40 gained 0.4% to 8,437 on Tuesday, extending its winning streak to a third consecutive session and reaching its highest level in three weeks, as investors welcomed a flurry of corporate earnings and updates. Luxury heavyweight LVMH rose 0.5% after reporting an acceleration in second-quarter sales growth and topping first-half revenue and earnings expectations. The upbeat update lifted sector peers, with Hermès gaining 1.8% and Kering rising 2.1%. Moreover, Safran jumped 3.6% after raising its full-year guidance following a stronger-than-expected first half, as robust demand for civil aircraft engine spare parts helped drive its operating margin to a record 18.4%. Orange added 3.5% after posting solid first-half 2026 results and upgrading its guidance. On the economic front, French consumer confidence edged up to a four-month high of 86 in July from 84 in the previous month, although it remained below its long-term average of 100.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 gained 0.6% to 25,520 on Tuesday, its highest level since July 6, as investors welcomed corporate earnings updates and lower oil prices amid hopes the US and Iran could resume negotiations to ease tensions in the Middle East. Mercedes-Benz surged more than 5% to lead the index after reaffirming its passenger car margin target despite cutting its revenue forecast following a weak quarter. Meanwhile, TeamViewer fell over 2% after reporting another decline in revenue while maintaining its full-year guidance. AI-related stocks remained under pressure as concerns grew that the sector's rally has become overstretched, alongside rising competition from China and increasing funding requirements. Infineon and Siemens Energy both slipped around 1%, while Hochtief dropped 3%, with the construction firm exposed through its US subsidiary Turner to slowing enthusiasm around large data center projects.

The FTSE 100 traded modestly higher on Tuesday, with strong gains in consumer stocks offsetting weakness in banks and energy shares. Unilever surged more than 6% after the consumer goods group reported better-than-expected results, driven by robust performance in the US and emerging markets, while also raising its outlook for 2026. Barclays was among the biggest laggards, falling more than 5% despite reporting a 16% increase in second-quarter group income. The lender benefited from strong investment banking and US consumer operations amid heightened market volatility, but the results prompted profit-taking and weighed on the broader banking sector. Energy stocks also remained under pressure as oil prices extended recent losses. Shell slipped 0.8% and BP eased 0.4% after US President Donald Trump said there was a good chance of reaching an agreement with Iran, raising expectations of easing geopolitical tensions and reducing concerns over potential disruptions to global crude supplies.
