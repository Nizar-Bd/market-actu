US stocks rose on Friday with the S&P 500 gaining 0.7% and the Nasdaq rose 0.9% after the Supreme Court struck down President Trump’s reciprocal tariffs. Traders now face a new tug-of-war as the removal of original tariff uncertainty is met by fresh protectionist measures, with the White House vowing a stronger direction.

The CAC 40 surged 1.4% to a record 8,516 on Friday, capping its best week since early January after the US Supreme Court struck down President Trump’s global tariffs. The 6-3 ruling, which found the use of emergency powers to bypass Congress unconstitutional, sparked a massive relief rally in trade-sensitive luxury giants with LVMH jumping 4.4% and Hermes climbing 3.6%. Industrials also flourished on the news as Airbus rose 1.4% and Saint-Gobain added 3.1%. On the earnings front, Air Liquide surged 4.6% after reporting a 6.4% rise in 2025 net profit and raising its margin targets through 2027. This corporate optimism helped investors overlook mixed macro data, including a February composite PMI of 49.9 which signaled near-stagnation in the private sector. While energy major TotalEnergies fell 1.5% as oil prices cooled, banks like Societe Generale and BNP Paribas gained roughly 1% on the broader market strength. The index finished the week up approximately 1.3%.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 extended early gains to trade 0.8% higher around 25,230 on Friday, after the US Supreme Court struck down the reciprocal tariffs imposed by Donald Trump. The Court ruled that Trump exceeded his authority when he invoked emergency legislation to impose sweeping tariffs on dozens of trading partners, including the European Union. The decision triggered a rally in automotive stocks such as Porsche (2%), Mercedes-Benz (0.9%), and BMW (0.9%) and VW (0.4%), which have been particularly affected by US tariffs. Banks also advanced solidly, with Deutsche Bank and Commerzbank rising 1.7% and 1.2%, respectively. Meanwhile, traders assessed global economic data and the ongoing earnings season, while also keeping an eye on geopolitics. On the data front, a flash PMI survey revealed that German private sector activity performed better than expected in February, accelerating to a four-month peak. For the week, the index is set to rise about 1.3%.

The FTSE 100 rose more than 0.5% to close at a record 10,687 on Friday, driven by gains in banking and consumer goods stocks and a boost from the US Supreme Court striking down a large portion of President Trump’s global tariffs. Retail sales, preliminary February PMIs, and public finance data also supported the market, showing the UK economy in stronger shape. Retail sales posted their fastest growth in 20 months, signaling firm consumer demand, while Britain recorded a budget surplus aided by higher capital gains tax receipts and lower debt interest payments. Diageo gained 4%, BAT over 2%, HSBC 0.6%, Barclays 1.2%, Lloyds Banking 1.8%, Unilever 1.1%, and Relx 1.7%. For the week, the FTSE 100 rose 2.3%, marking its fourth consecutive weekly gain.

European stock indices closed sharply higher at new records on Friday, tracking the positive momentum for equity markets on favorable economic data and momentary relief from US sanctions. The Eurozone's STOXX 50 added 1.1% to 6,124 and the pan-European STOXX 600 rose 0.8% to 630. The US Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs that President Donald Trump had imposed on the European Union and all other countries in the reciprocal tariffs scheme. While the President vowed to reintroduce the tariffs under other programs, the outlook of lower trade barriers supported the sales outlook for industries with large exposure to the US. LVMH and Hermes added 4.5% and 3.8%, respectively, to lead the luxury sector, while Intesa Sanpaolo and ING rose more than 2% to set the pace for lenders. In the meantime, PMI data pointed to higher-than-expected economic activity in the Eurozone during February. Air Liquide jumped over 4% after posting strong results for the year.
