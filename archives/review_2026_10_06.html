US stock indices rose on Tuesday with support from the heavyweight tech sector as the surge in bond yields paused and softened pressure on the sector's credit outlook. The CAC 40 rose 0.4% to 7,865 on Tuesday, rebounding from the previous session amid easing oil prices and a pullback in global bond yields. Oil prices fell as resilient Middle Eastern crude exports and a G7 emergency stockpile release eased supply concerns, reducing energy-driven inflation pressures. Yields also eased from multi-year highs, offering some respite to credit-sensitive markets. Financials posted solid gains, with BNP Paribas up 2.5% and AXA adding 1.5%. Luxury and industrial stocks were mixed as concerns over France’s high debt levels and political uncertainty lingered. The government sought to ease market jitters by unveiling its 2027 budget. The state budget deficit widened to €159.6 billion in January-August from €157.5 billion a year earlier. LVMH added 1%, while Hermes shed 0.5%. Among industrials, STMicroelectronics gained 2.5%, tracking strength in US chipmakers. Schneider Electric lost 3.8%, extending Monday’s losses after announcing its acquisition of PTC.

The DAX closed 0.8% higher at 25,449 on Tuesday, extending gains for a third consecutive session and reaching its highest level in around two weeks. The index tracked a broadly positive mood across European and US markets, supported by lower oil prices and bond yields. Concerns over France’s fiscal position remained a headwind for the broader market, however, alongside political uncertainty surrounding a snap election in Spain. Expectations for further ECB rate hikes have also receded, with markets now pricing in a nearly 80% probability of another rate increase by the end of the year. SAP (2.2%), Infineon (1.9%), Siemens (1.5%) and Siemens Energy (1.4%) posted strong gains, while Rheinmetall (-2.8%) was the worst performer on the DAX.

The FTSE 100 closed 0.4% higher at 10,542 on Tuesday, extending the rebound from the three-month low of 10,428 on Thursday and tracking a strong session for global equities after a pause in the current bond rout. European stocks closed firmly higher on Tuesday with support from banks, consumer discretionary companies, and auto producers as sovereign yields paused their recent surge. The Euro STOXX 50 added 0.5% to 6,274 and the STOXX Europe 600 gained 0.5% to 636.6. Long-maturity euro-area sovereign yields were mostly lower on the session, especially in economies with higher debt pressure, softening concerns that the ECB would be forced to intervene against fragmentation. Banks tracked the rebound in credit, with BNP Paribas jumping 2.5% while UniCredit, Santander, and Deutsche Bank added 1.5% each. Intesa Sanpaolo also gained 1.3%, rebounding from yesterday's drop as markets reconsidered how a potential acquisition of Banca MPS could impact future returns. On the other hand, Schneider Electric sank 4.5% after agreeing to buy US software firm PTC in a $22.6 billion deal.
