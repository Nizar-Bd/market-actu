Futures tracking US equity indices were higher on Monday after sharp losses last week, as the chip sector stabilized. The CAC 40 traded little changed around 8,342 on Monday, opening the week on a cautious footing as investors weighed renewed upward pressure on oil prices amid escalating tensions in the Middle East. The conflict intensified after the US carried out a ninth consecutive day of strikes against Iran, while Tehran responded with attacks on targets across the region. The latest developments underscored the fragility of the ceasefire reached last month, raising concerns that a prolonged conflict could disrupt energy supplies, fuel inflationary pressures, and complicate the outlook for interest rates. Among individual stocks, LVMH (-0.6%), Airbus (-1.5%), Safran (-1.4%), and AXA (-0.5%) declined. In contrast, energy giant TotalEnergies advanced 1.7%, benefiting from the sharp rise in crude oil prices.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 edged higher to around 24,900 on Monday, attempting to recover after two consecutive weeks of losses driven by a sharp correction in AI-related stocks and escalating tensions between the US and Iran. Brent crude climbed to its highest level in more than a month after the two countries exchanged a series of retaliatory strikes, fueling inflation concerns. Investors are also looking ahead to Thursday's European Central Bank policy meeting, where rates are widely expected to remain unchanged following June's hike. However, markets continue to price in two additional ECB rate increases by early 2027, with the first potentially coming as soon as September. On the corporate front, the automotive industry is in focus after Tesla announced plans to rapidly expand production at its Grünheide plant near Berlin, while Salzgitter extended Friday's gains after the steelmaker raised its full-year earnings forecast.

The FTSE 100 fell more than 0.5% on Monday as renewed Middle East tensions lifted oil prices and weighed on broader market sentiment. Investors also focused on Andy Burnham, who is set to become the UK's new prime minister, marking the country's seventh leader in just over a decade. Banking stocks were among the main laggards, with HSBC, Lloyds Banking, Barclays, NatWest and Standard Chartered falling between 0.4% and 0.8%. Segro dropped around 2% after rejecting a third takeover proposal from US logistics property group Prologis, which valued the company at about £13.5 billion. Unilever declined 1.6%, Rolls Royce lost 1%, while AstraZeneca, GSK and BAT also traded lower. In contrast, energy majors Shell and BP outperformed as higher crude prices supported the sector.

European stocks opened the week on a cautious note, with the STOXX 50 trading little changed while the STOXX 600 slipped 0.2%. Investors returned from the weekend to renewed upward pressure on oil prices as the conflict in the Middle East continued to escalate. The US launched a ninth consecutive day of strikes against Iran, while Iran responded with attacks on targets across the region. Meanwhile, shipping activity through the Strait of Hormuz remained severely constrained. Markets are also preparing for a busy week of corporate earnings. Travel and leisure were among the worst performing sectors while energy outperformed. Ryanair shares fell around 6% to bottom the STOXX 600 after the airline reported a 34% decline in profit. Novartis (-1.1%), Nestle (-1.2%), Unilever (-1.5%), Airbus (-1%) and Mercedes-Benz (-0.9%) were also lower. On the other hand, ASML Holding (0.8%), TotalEnergies (1.4%), and Siemens Energy (2.5%) booked gains.
