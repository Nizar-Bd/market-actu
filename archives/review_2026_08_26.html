The CAC 40 climbed 0.5% to 8,483 on Wednesday, ending two straight sessions of losses as hopes for a near-term reopening of the Strait of Hormuz boosted risk appetite. Oil prices eased after Iran said it had resumed discussions with Oman on managing traffic through the strategic waterway, after heightened economic pressure from US President Trump amid the broader conflict. However, investor caution lingered ahead of Fitch's rating review of France on August 28, while markets also closely watched budget negotiations and the approaching presidential election cycle. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu’s minority government is expected to unveil its full budget proposal in late September, but the fiscal outlook remains uncertain as political attention increasingly shifts toward the highly contested presidential race. Luxury stocks led the advance, particularly LVMH (1.2%), Hermès (1.5%), and Kering (1.8%). Other gainers included Airbus (1.3%), Safran (1.6%), and Saint-Gobain (1.2%).

The DAX 40 turned slightly positive, trading around 26,300 on Wednesday and heading for a second straight session of gains. Market sentiment was supported by lower oil prices amid hopes for the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, alongside stabilizing bond yields. However, a sense of caution prevailed ahead of Nvidia’s earnings and key US inflation data that could influence rate expectations. Banks led sectoral gains, with tech, industrials, pharma and healthcare, utilities, and chemical stocks also advancing, offsetting sharp losses in software. Deutsche Bank and Commerzbank surged 4.3% and 2.9%, respectively. Qiagen NV shares rose 2.4% amid renewed rumours that the company is in talks with EQT and KKR over a potential €50-per-share sale. Chipmaker Infineon Technologies added 0.8%. On the flip side, software giant SAP underperformed, dropping 3.6%, after UBS downgraded the stock to Neutral from Buy, citing slow progress in agency AI and weaker cloud backlog growth.

The FTSE 100 traded slightly lower on Wednesday, ending a six-session winning streak as weaker oil prices weighed on energy stocks. Entain and Persimmon were also in focus after FTSE Russell indicated that both companies are set to be removed from the FTSE 100 in its latest reshuffle. Meanwhile, Ofgem said the UK energy price cap will rise 4% from October, citing higher wholesale gas prices linked to the Middle East conflict. Investor attention is also turning to Nvidia’s results, with markets looking for further signals on the strength of the AI investment cycle.
