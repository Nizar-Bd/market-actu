US stocks were sharply higher on Thursday, erasing losses from the last wo sessions lower oil prices softened Treasury yields. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 gained over 1% while the Dow rose 0.7%. Frankfurt’s DAX 40 rose about 0.7% to above 25,700 on Thursday, reaching its highest level in more than a week as oil prices continued to decline and global bond yields eased following the widely anticipated 25-basis-point rate hike by the US Federal Reserve. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh maintained a firm stance on inflation while avoiding a renewed surge in long-term yields, a combination that reassured investors. Meanwhile, trade tensions were in focus after President Trump threatened the EU with “very serious tariffs” or trade restrictions over Ursula von der Leyen’s proposal to allow Canada to become the bloc’s first “associate member.” Most sectors traded in the green, led by autos and industrials. Energy stocks such as Siemens Energy (+2%) and automotive stocks like BMW (+1.5%) and Volkswagen (1.1%) saw higher demand. On the flip side, the tech sector came under pressure, with Infineon Technologies among the biggest decliners, falling 2.5%.

The FTSE 100 gained ground and rose 0.5% to the 10,750 mark on Thursday after the Bank of England maintained its rate unchanged, as expected by the City. Three members of the Bank's Monetary Policy Council opted for hikes, also aligned with expectations, as policymakers heeded to the impact of higher energy prices on underlying inflation. In turn, equity liquidity was supported by a softer-than-expected unwinding of the BoE balance sheet, lending support to stocks and Gilts. Financial companies hence led the rise in the session, with HSBC and Standard Chartered gaining over 1% each, while 3i added nearly 2%. Meanwhile, SSE rose over 2% as natural gas costs fell for a second session in wholesale markets. Lastly, Next rose 1% after citing a modest improvement in sales expectations and additional cost savings, particularly in warehousing.

European stocks moved higher on Thursday, with both the STOXX 50 and STOXX 600 gaining 0.7%, tracking a broader global rally after the Fed raised interest rates by 25bps, as expected, and signalled at least one further hike this year. Chair Warsh also reaffirmed the central bank’s commitment to tackling inflation, helping to restore investor confidence in its policy stance. Bond yields retreated and oil prices edged lower, providing additional relief to market sentiment. Meanwhile, US President Trump threatened to impose tariffs on the EU, or potentially halt trade with the bloc altogether, if it proceeds with its plan to make Canada its first-ever “associate member”. The auto sector was among the top performers, led by Stellantis (+2.5%), Renault (+2.1%) and BMW (1.9%), while tech stocks also advanced, with ASML gaining 2%. Sodexo surged nearly 4% after JPMorgan upgraded the stock to “overweight” from “neutral”.
