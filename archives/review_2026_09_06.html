US stocks closed mostly lower on Friday after stronger-than-expected labor market data gave the Fed more room to raise interest rates. The CAC 40 inched 0.1% lower to close at 8,279 on Friday following the release of domestic and Eurozone economic data. Eurozone retail sales fell 0.6% in July, following a 0.2% increase in June and missing market expectations for a 0.3% rise. Luxury stocks closed mixed, with Hermes losing 1.6% and LVMH shedding 0.4%, while L’Oréal rose 1.4% and Kering edged 0.2% higher. France’s construction activity recorded its sharpest contraction since May 2020, while overall Eurozone construction activity contracted at a faster pace than in July. Despite this, Vinci gained 0.7%, while Eiffage edged 0.3% lower. TotalEnergies also weighed on the index, dropping 2.5% as oil prices pulled back. Dassault Systèmes tumbled 6.5%, tracking weakness in AI-exposed hyperscalers on Wall Street. Industrials were mostly higher. Legrand rose 3.3% after Deutsche Bank raised its price target to €160 from €150. STMicroelectronics gained 1.8%, tracking strength among US chipmakers. The index lost 1.3% on the week.

The DAX 40 closed about 0.2% up at 26,048 on Friday, marking the second day of gains, powered by the automotive sector. Technology stocks and industrials also provided support. Volkswagen shares jumped over 6%, following the board’s approval of its biggest restructuring plan in 89 years. The plan includes 50,000 job cuts and alternatives for four German plants, while also reducing the supervisory board’s influence on key decisions, where unions and Lower Saxony hold a majority. BMW (1.9%), Continental (1.9%) and Mercedes-Benz Group (1%) advanced solidly. Semiconductor stocks also attracted buying interest, with Infineon up 2.4%. Meanwhile, investors remained focused on the interest-rate outlook, while geopolitical tensions in the Middle East persisted. The ECB is expected to raise rates by 25 basis points next week, while a hotter-than-expected US jobs report has also revived expectations for further rate hikes. The index posted a weekly decline of roughly 2%.

London’s FTSE 100 pared losses to close virtually flat at 10,831 on Friday, with traders digesting the latest US jobs report that strengthened bets on policy tightening. In the UK, gilt yields remain elevated, with long-dated yields recently reaching multi-decade highs. BoE Chief Economist Huw Pill said that higher rates now could limit the risk of more aggressive tightening later to tackle inflation fuelled by the Iran war. Experian slipped over 4%, making it the worst performer, after FHFA Director Bill Pulte accused credit agencies of overcharging consumers and flagged possible reforms to boost competition. Coca-Cola Europacific Partners and RELX followed, down around 2% each. On the upside, Computacenter rallied 4.1% after UBS raised its price target, while Vodafone gained 2.7% after Goldman Sachs upgraded the stock to ‘buy’ from ‘sell’ and lifted its price target to 155p from 85p. For the week, the index shed about 0.1%.
