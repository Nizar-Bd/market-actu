The CAC 40 rose 0.4% to 8,485 on Tuesday, rebounding from losses in the previous session as stabilization in technology sector helped improve risk sentiment. Investor attention turned to Nvidia’s closely watched earnings report due Wednesday, with the results expected to offer a crucial gauge of the strength and longevity of the AI-driven market. Investors also continued to assess developments surrounding US-Iran tensions after Washington's threat of an "economic D-Day" through tougher sanctions on Iran failed to generate the level of market disruption many had expected. Domestically, the consumer confidence index remained unchanged at 86 in August, falling short of forecasts for 87 and staying well below its long-run average of 100, highlighting ongoing caution among households. Notable gainers included Airbus (1.4%), Safran (1.5%), and Legrand (1.1%). Meanwhile, luxury stocks lagged the broader market, with LVMH down 0.7%, Hermès slipping 0.3%, and Kering losing 1.1%.

The DAX 40 extended initial gains, rising 0.8% to cross 26,300 on Tuesday, following a downbeat session in the prior day, amid some relief from the latest US sanctions on Iran. Positive domestic GDP and business confidence data also boosted sentiment. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced Monday a global sanctions program targeting countries that maintain economic and trade ties with Iran, although the measures were seen as too vague. In the meantime, chip stocks attempted to stabilize following a weak performance on Monday, with investors positioning ahead of Nvidia’s earnings report. Infineon Technologies led gains, rising more than 2%, along with AI-related stocks. Siemens Energy advanced 1.4%, also buoyed by reports that the company had commissioned Goldman Sachs to prepare for a potential sale of its majority stake in its steam turbine business. Among top losers were Siemens Healthineers (-1.1%), SAP (-01.1%) and Volkswagen (-0.7%).

The FTSE 100 traded higher on Tuesday, extending its winning streak to six sessions, its longest run of gains since May. Melrose was among the strongest performers, rising more than 8% after the aerospace company announced a $100 million claims programme related to damage at its GKN Aerospace facility in California. A criminal investigation into the incident has been closed, while the site is expected to resume operations next month. Oil prices were only slightly lower after the US unveiled new sanctions targeting businesses involved in Iran’s trade network. The measures, dubbed “Operation Economic Outcast” by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, have affected companies in China and Hong Kong but have so far avoided major Chinese financial institutions. Meanwhile, weakness in both precious and industrial metals weighed on miners.
