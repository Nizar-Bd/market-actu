US stock indices closed higher on Friday after weaker-than-expected labor market data reduced expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike next month. The S&P 500 rose 0.6% to a record close of 7,758, the Nasdaq gained 1.3%, and the Dow added 152 points. Nonfarm payrolls unexpectedly fell by 23,000 from the previous month, while both the unemployment rate and labor force participation rate declined, easing concerns that labor market strength could fuel inflation. Lower Treasury yields supported credit-sensitive stocks. SpaceX surged 15.8%, extending Thursday's rally after Argus Research upgraded the stock to "Buy," citing rapid returns on its AI infrastructure investments. Palantir jumped 10.3%, extending gains following strong second-quarter results and a higher 2026 outlook. Meanwhile, Sandisk and Western Digital fell 3.7% and 3.8%, respectively, while Berkshire Hathaway slipped ahead of its earnings report.

The CAC 40 edged up 0.2% to close at a record 8,715 on Friday, supported by better-than-expected trade data and gains in US technology stocks. France's trade deficit narrowed more than expected to €5.8 billion in June from €7.9 billion in May, as exports rose 2.6% month-on-month to €54.5 billion. Industrial stocks advanced, with Airbus up 0.5%, Safran adding 0.6%, and Sanofi gaining 1.3%. Technology shares also moved higher, with STMicroelectronics rising 3.2% and Schneider Electric advancing 1% amid sector gains on Wall Street. In contrast, luxury stocks mostly declined, with LVMH down 0.3% and Kering and L'Oréal both falling 1.1% as Middle East tensions weighed on risk appetite. TotalEnergies also slipped 0.6% amid volatile oil prices. Broader sectors were mixed as France's unemployment rate rose to 8.3% in Q2 2026 from 8.1% in the previous quarter, reaching its highest level since Q3 2020.

The DAX 40 extended early gains to close about 0.7% firmer at 26,355 on Friday, setting a fresh record high and booking a 2.8% weekly gain. Market sentiment remained supported by an upbeat earnings season, while investors awaited developments in US-Iran negotiations to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Germany's stronger-than-expected industrial production and export data further reinforced the positive market tone. Technology shares rallied, with strength across software and semiconductor stocks. SAP climbed 4.1% and Infineon Technologies advanced 2.9%. Scout24, Siemens, BMW and Deutsche Borse also performed well, rising between 1.7% and 3.6%. On the flip side, Daimler Truck (-2.8%) led losses after reporting an 18% drop in Q2 profit, despite raising full-year adjusted EBIT guidance. Munchener Ruck fell 1.4% even after a suprise 6% increase in net profit. Allianz fell 0.6%, as non-life insurance growth disappointed despite record operating profit.

The FTSE 100 edged higher on Friday, remaining within a narrow ±0.5% range as investors stayed cautious amid uncertainty over a lasting agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Precious metals miners outperformed, with Fresnillo and Endeavour gaining 5.3% and 4.1%, respectively, while Rio Tinto and Anglo American added around 1%-1.5%. Diageo climbed more than 3%, extending Thursday’s 5.6% gain following its restructuring announcement. In contrast, Shell and BP fell as oil prices fluctuated and remained lower for the week. JD Sports rose more than 2% after appointing Peter Agnefjäll as chairman from September 1. Meanwhile, Lloyds reported that UK house prices were unchanged in July, with annual growth slowing to 0.1%, its weakest pace since November 2023. Elsewhere, weaker US jobs data reduced expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike next month. The FTSE 100 finished slightly higher for the week.
