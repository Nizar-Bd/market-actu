US stocks inched higher on Wednesday, pausing the week's pullback as markets assessed how the economy may fare against higher interest rates. Chip producers and hardware companies extended yesterday's rebound after concerns over AI safety risks voiced by Anthropic and OpenAI jeopardized the momentum of soaring hyperscaler capital expenditure. The CAC 40 rose 0.4% to 8,118 on Wednesday, rebounding from a nearly four-month low hit in the previous session as weaker oil prices and lower bond yields supported equities. Frankfurt's DAX 40 rose 0.4% to surpass 25,490 on Wednesday, halting two sessions of losses, as easing oil prices and lower global bond yields offered some relief. This was despite ongoing tensions in the Middle East. Meanwhile, the focus turned to the Fed’s policy outcome later in the day, with a 25-basis-point rate hike already fully priced in. The tech sector provided the main support, alongside banks, utilities and industrials, offsetting weakness in software, autos and retailers. Shares of companies benefiting from the AI boom posted the strongest gains, led by Hochtief, Infineon Technologies and Siemens Energy, which rose between 1.7% and 3.2%. Commerzbank and Deutsche Bank advanced 1.8% and 1.4%, respectively. On the downside, top losers included SAP (-1.7%), Zalando (-1.2%), Volkswagen (-1.1%) and Mercedes-Benz Group (-0.7%).

The FTSE 100 traded 0.5% higher on Wednesday after falling 0.4% in the previous session, as easing oil prices supported sentiment despite a fresh rise in UK inflation. Consumer prices increased 3.1% year-on-year in August, up from 2.9% in July and matching market expectations, although above the Bank of England’s 2.8% forecast. Higher motor-fuel prices, linked to the ongoing Iran conflict, and increased airfares contributed to the acceleration. Markets are now focused on the Federal Reserve’s decision later today and the Bank of England’s meeting on Thursday. Barratt Redrow surged more than 7% after reporting stronger-than-expected annual results, while Persimmon gained 3%. Banks including Standard Chartered, NatWest, Lloyds Banking Group and HSBC all rose more than 1.5% after JPMorgan forecast stronger third-quarter trading and investment-banking revenue. Entain fell more than 1% following reports that UK officials are considering higher taxes on slot machines.

European stocks rose for the first time in three sessions on Wednesday, with the STOXX 50 gaining 0.6% and the STOXX 600 adding 0.4%, as a modest retreat in oil prices and stable bond yields offered investors some relief and a breather. Meanwhile, traders eagerly awaited the Fed’s monetary policy decision, with markets pricing in roughly a 93% probability of a 25bps increase in the fed funds rate. The banking sector was among the top gainers, including HSBC (+1.5%), Banco Santander (+0.8%), BNP Paribas (+1.0%), and Standard Chartered (+1.7%). HSML Holding also advanced 2.2%, while Barratt Redrow surged nearly 8% to top the STOXX 600, after its full-year results beat expectations despite trimming its home completions target for fiscal 2027. On the downside, Marks & Spencer fell 4.3% to the bottom of the main stock index. SAP (-1.2%) and L’Oréal (-1.3%) also traded lower.
