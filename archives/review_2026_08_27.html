US equity indices closed higher on Thursday as strong results from Nvidia supported the technology sector. The S&P 500 rose 0.7%, the Nasdaq advanced 1.4% and the Dow added 105 points. Nvidia surged 8.7% after strong demand for AI computing drove the world’s largest company to forecast a 70% increase in revenue next fiscal year, challenging persistent skepticism that capital spending on AI infrastructure would slow. The outlook supported other companies exposed to AI infrastructure, with Broadcom up 4.5% and Intel adding 4.4%. AI also boosted hyperscalers, with Microsoft up 1.7% and Oracle adding 2.1%. Software infrastructure stocks rallied, with Palantir up 4.7%, Salesforce soaring 22.6%, CrowdStrike surging 20.5% and Palo Alto jumping 12.8%. Broader sectors were lower amid an unfavorable macroeconomic backdrop. Long-term yields remained elevated amid widening federal deficits, soaring AI-related debt issuance, foreign trade tensions, and the risk of higher energy-driven inflation.

The CAC 40 tumbled 1.7% to close at 8,319 on Thursday, falling to its lowest level in over a month ahead of Fitch’s review of France’s sovereign credit rating. Fitch’s assessment is due Friday, after the agency downgraded France to A+ last year. Political uncertainty also weighed on sentiment ahead of an early presidential debate. France faces a particularly challenging budget battle in the coming months, with one of the largest deficits in the eurozone. The budget dispute and presidential race are expected to fuel uncertainty. Losses were broad-based amid heightened risk aversion, with LVMH down 1.4%, Safran shedding 2%, Engie losing 3.4% and Vinci retreating 3.9%. Financials underperformed, with BNP Paribas losing 4.8%, Crédit Agricole down 4% and Société Générale shedding 5%. Pernod Ricard fell 4.6% after reporting lower sales in FY 2026.

The DAX 40 closed about 0.3% up at 26,367 on Thursday, the highest level since mid-August, marking the third session of gains. Tech stocks, including chipmakers and software firms, led the advance, after Nvidia’s strong sales outlook reinforced optimism over continued AI spending. SAP was the standout performer, climbing 5.2%, while Infineon Technologies added 4%. Autos also posted solid gains, led by BMW (+3.9%), Volkswagen (+3.4%) and Mercedes-Benz Group (+2.1%). Meanwhile, investors remained cautious amid geopolitical uncertainties and ahead of the Jackson Hole central bankers’ symposium, which kicks off today, with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh scheduled to speak on Friday. The ECB’s meeting Accounts suggested that a rate hike could be needed in September, with policymakers highlighting upside risks to inflation and downside risks to growth stemming from the Middle East.

European stocks closed sharply lower on Thursday amid sharp pressure from banks and industrial giants. The Euro STOXX 50 fell 0.75% to 6,422 and the STOXX Europe 600 fell 0.7% to 651.7.
