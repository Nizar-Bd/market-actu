Frankfurt's DAX 40 climbed 0.8% to 25,800 on the final trading session of July, reaching its highest level since the record peak set earlier this month and putting the index on track for a monthly gain of more than 3%. The advance was driven by a strong rebound in technology stocks, broadly upbeat corporate earnings, and a continued sharp decline in oil prices, despite persistent geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. Chipmaker Infineon led the benchmark with a gain of more than 6%, followed by Siemens Energy and Hochtief, both of which advanced 3.7%. On the downside, Siemens Healthineers fell more than 2% after lowering its fiscal 2025/26 revenue guidance due to continued weakness in its diagnostics business. Elsewhere, defense electronics company Hensoldt reported that order intake more than doubled year over year, sportswear manufacturer Puma reaffirmed its full-year guidance despite weaker demand, and wafer producer Siltronic posted strong quarterly results.

The FTSE 100 climbed more than 0.5% on Friday to a fresh record high near 10,970, putting the index on track for a weekly gain of around 2.2% and a monthly advance of roughly 4.5%, its strongest performance since February. Investors continued to monitor weaker oil prices and renewed volatility in semiconductor stocks. NatWest jumped nearly 4% after raising its full-year guidance and announcing plans for a share buyback alongside its 2026 annual results. J Sainsbury gained more than 3% after agreeing to sell its Argos business to Swift Partners for at least £120 million. On the downside, Pearson fell almost 4% despite reaffirming its outlook, while IG Group dropped over 5% after announcing a deal worth up to $1.3 billion to acquire US sports prediction platform Underdog. IAG lost around 1.6% after missing revenue estimates and warning of higher fuel costs and prolonged Middle East route suspensions.
