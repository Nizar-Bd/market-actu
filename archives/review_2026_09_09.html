The CAC 40 fell 0.6% to 8,269 on Wednesday, reversing the previous session’s gains and hitting its lowest level since July, as escalating Middle East tensions kept risk appetite in check. Oil prices remained elevated after Iran said it had struck two US vessels and eight oil tankers in the Gulf in retaliation for American attacks on five Iranian tankers near Kharg Island. The latest developments heightened inflation concerns and reinforced expectations that the European Central Bank will raise interest rates by 25 basis points at Thursday’s policy meeting. Domestically, industrial production slipped 0.4% month-on-month in July, defying market expectations for a 0.3% rise and extending June’s revised 0.1% decline. Luxury stocks led the retreat, with LVMH sliding 1.4% to its lowest level since November 2020 and Kering losing 2.3%. Elsewhere, Airbus fell 1.6% after cutting its long-term demand outlook for single-aisle aircraft in China.

The DAX 40 fell 0.5% to around 25,870 on Wednesday, the lowest level in a week, mirroring a negative sentiment across Europe. The conflict between the US and Iran over control of the Strait of Hormuz has escalated further, pushing oil prices even higher and fuelling concerns over inflation and interest rates. The US military said it destroyed five Iranian crude oil tankers and sank one of them in retaliation for Tehran's attack on a US warship. Meanwhile, the ECB is widely expected to raise rates by 25 bps tomorrow, with further hikes possible before year-end. Energy-intensive industrials and banks were among the hardest hit. Rheinmetall slipped 3%, making it the worst performer, reversing the gains of the prior day. Beiersdorf dropped nearly 2% after Deutsche Bank downgraded the stock from “Hold” to “Sell.” Deutsche Börse declined 1.7% as investors reacted to its plans to issue a €600 million convertible bond to finance general corporate purposes and liabilities.

The FTSE 100 traded at a one-week low on Wednesday as Brent crude surged through $100 a barrel for the first time since July, with renewed fighting in the Middle East keeping upward pressure on oil prices. The US destroyed five Iranian crude-carrying tankers after an attempt to strike a US Navy warship with ballistic missiles, while Tehran subsequently fired missiles towards Jordan and warned vessels in the Persian Gulf. Energy stocks were among the few gainers, with Shell up 1.1% and BP rising 1.8%. Utilities also advanced, with SSE, Centrica, United Utilities, Severn Trent and National Grid gaining between 1.1% and 1.6%. Aberdeen Group rose more than 2% after appointing Torbjörn Magnusson as its new chairman, subject to regulatory approval. Magnusson is the former chairman of Nordea and car insurer Hastings. Elsewhere, financials, consumer staples, industrials and consumer discretionary stocks were mostly lower.

Stocks in Europe were lower on Wednesday, with the STOXX 50 falling 0.7% and the STOXX 600 declining 0.5%, as higher oil prices continued to weigh on market sentiment. Brent crude hit $100 a barrel as tensions between the US and Iran escalated. The rise in energy prices could put further pressure on inflation and reinforce the case for additional monetary policy tightening. The ECB is widely expected to deliver a 25bps rate hike tomorrow, with traders set to assess any further guidance on the policy outlook. Inditex tumbled more than 3% despite reporting a better-than-expected start to its autumn trading season, with currency-adjusted sales rising 9% in August. Rheinmetall was also among the worst performers, falling 2.5% and nearly reversing its 3% gain in the previous session. Energy stocks, meanwhile, moved higher, with TotalEnergies gaining 1%, Shell rising 1%, BP adding 1.7%, Galp advancing 1.6%, and Repsol climbing 2.1%.
