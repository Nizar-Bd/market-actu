US stock indices pulled back from session highs on Tuesday as Treasuries reversed gains and pressured AI infrastructure names alongside major earnings. The FTSE 100 pared earlier gains and edged down on Tuesday, after two days of gains, as renewed concerns over the sustainability of the artificial intelligence rally weighed on global markets after the US open. The pullback in chipmakers overshadowed strong earnings from major US banks, although London’s limited exposure to technology stocks helped it outperform several European peers. London Stock Exchange Group fell nearly 5% after reports emerged that it was in discussions to acquire credit information platform Octus from UK private equity firm Permira, a development the company confirmed. Mining shares also came under pressure, with Antofagasta dropping 5%, Rio Tinto losing 2% and Anglo American declining 1.6%. BP provided some support, rising more than 3% as investors responded to the company’s progress in reducing its debt burden. The oil major cut net debt by approximately $8 billion over the past three months, highlighting a significant improvement in its balance sheet.

European stocks erased early gains and closed firmly lower on Tuesday amid sharp losses for the AI infrastructure sector and heavyweight industrials. The Euro STOXX 50 fell 0.6% to 6,170 and the STOXX Europe 600 eased 0.2% to 635. ASML slid 4.2% ahead of its earnings report this week, tracking sharp losses for chip producers across major Western economies after Chinese chipmaker CXMT said it achieved a breakthrough in its memory architecture that rivals Korean and US competitors. Meanwhile, LVMH closed slightly lower after its fashion and leather goods revenue fell 5% year-over-year in the third quarter due to slower sales in China. The developments prompted analysts and brokers to downgrade recommendations on Hermès, pushing the stock 2% lower. On the other hand, banks were mostly higher, with UniCredit and Nordea adding nearly 1%.
