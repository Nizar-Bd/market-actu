US stock futures rose slightly on Monday as investors continued to monitor escalations in the Iran war and surging oil prices. Markets are also assessing reports that the US will soon announce a coalition of countries to escort ships through the narrow waterway linking the Persian Gulf with global markets. Last week, the Dow fell 1.99%, the S&P 500 dropped 1.6% and the Nasdaq Composite lost 1.26%, with all three benchmarks declining for a third straight week as geopolitical risks weighed on sentiment. Higher energy prices and rising inflationary pressures also reduced expectations that the Federal Reserve will cut interest rates.

The FTSE 100 traded 0.3% higher on Monday, attempting to recover after three straight sessions of losses, supported by comments from President Trump who increased pressure on other nations to help reopen the Strait of Hormuz and said the US is in talks with Iran. The remarks appear to be offsetting broader uncertainty in markets. During a recent call with G7 leaders, Trump was repeatedly asked about his strategy for the conflict with Iran but declined to give details, saying he had several options in mind and wanted the conflict to end soon. Energy stocks led gains with BP up 1.7% and Shell rising 1.3% as Brent crude remained elevated just below 105 dollars per barrel. Other heavyweights including HSBC, Unilever, Rolls Royce and BAT gained more than 0.5%. Travel and leisure stocks lagged with IAG and Intercontinental Hotels down about 1.5%, while miners Fresnillo (-1.3%) and Antofagasta (-1%) fell as gold and copper prices extended losses for a fourth session.
