The CAC 40 hovered near the flatline at around 8,687 on Thursday, following the previous session's decline as investors remained cautious amid conflicting signals over efforts to secure a lasting ceasefire between the US and Iran. While reports indicated that Washington and Tehran had agreed to extend a 60-day ceasefire brokered by Pakistan, tensions remained elevated after Iran threatened to prolong the conflict until the end of President Donald Trump's term and again rejected his claims that the US has "total control" over the Strait of Hormuz. Offsetting some geopolitical concerns, softer-than-expected US inflation data reinforced expectations that the Federal Reserve will keep interest rates unchanged at its September meeting. Among the index heavyweights, gains were led by LVMH (0.5%), L'Oréal (0.4%), TotalEnergies (0.5%), and Hermès (0.9%). In contrast, STMicroelectronics (-1%), Dassault Systèmes (-0.9%), and Air Liquide (-0.4%) were among the notable decliners.

The DAX 40 edged higher to fresh record levels around 26,460 on Thursday, following losses in the prior day, led by gains in banks, utilities and industrials. Oil prices moved lower despite geopolitical uncertainty, providing some relief from concerns over energy supply disruptions and inflation. Meanwhile, investors eyed more corporate earnings and economic data from Europe and the US. RWE was among the top performers, rising more than 1%, as it reported better-than-expected first-half profit, helped by a Dutch government compensation payment and stronger energy trading, while reaffirming its 2026 dividend and long-term profit targets. Thyssenkrupp also advanced after its adjusted EBIT rose to €183 million in the third quarter, prompting the company to raise its full-year EBIT floor to €600 million from €500 million. Rheinmetall, Qiagen, Siemens Energy and MTU Aero Engines also posted solid gains, alongside Commerzbank and Deutsche Bank.

The FTSE 100 traded lower on Thursday, extending its decline for a fourth consecutive session and underperforming other European markets, as weakness in commodity-related stocks outweighed stronger-than-expected UK economic data. UK GDP rose 0.4% in the second quarter, in line with forecasts, while monthly growth accelerated to 0.3% in June, beating expectations. However, falling commodity prices weighed heavily on the index. Shell and BP declined 0.4% and 1%, respectively, while miners suffered steeper losses as gold, silver and copper prices retreated. Antofagasta fell more than 5%, Rio Tinto dropped 4.5% and Anglo American declined 3%. Precious-metal miners Fresnillo and Endeavour also lost more than 3% each. Meanwhile, Entain reported stronger-than-expected operational profitability and reaffirmed that it remains comfortable with its full-year expectations.

European stocks traded close to record highs on Thursday, supported by a modest decline in oil and gas prices that helped ease concerns over energy costs. The STOXX 50 gained around 0.5%, while the broader STOXX 600 added 0.2%. Stabilising energy prices improved investor sentiment and reduced some of the pressure on inflation expectations. UK economic data also offered encouragement, showing that the region’s economies have so far proved resilient to the energy shock. UK GDP expanded by 0.4% in the second quarter, while growth strengthened in June. Corporate earnings provided an additional boost. Danish shipping giant Maersk surged around 8% after significantly exceeding profit expectations and raising its full-year earnings guidance, reflecting strong performance in its core businesses. Meanwhile, Entain and Danish offshore wind developer Orsted also reported first-half core profits above expectations.
