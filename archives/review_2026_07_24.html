The CAC 40 was little changed around 8,303 on Friday but remained on track for a weekly loss as investors weighed escalating Middle East tensions and fresh US trade measures. With the US carrying out its 13th consecutive day of strikes on Iran, President Trump threatened further military action against Iran and the Houthis over any additional attacks on Red Sea shipping. On the trade front, Trump imposed tariffs ranging from 10% to 12.5% on imports from major trading partners over alleged failures to curb forced labor in supply chains, though the EU tariff rate is expected to remain capped at 10%. Domestically, preliminary data showed private-sector activity improved to a five-month high of 49.6 in July, as stronger services activity (49.8 vs 46.8) offset a deeper manufacturing downturn (48.8 vs 49.1). Among stocks, LVMH (-0.3%), Sanofi (-2.6%), and Christian Dior (-1.1%) declined, while banks outperformed, led by BNP Paribas (1.2%), Crédit Agricole (1.5%), and Société Générale (0.9%).

Frankfurt's DAX 40 rose 0.6% to around 24,900 on Friday, rebounding from a near one-month low as investors digested strong corporate earnings and upbeat PMI data despite escalating Middle East tensions and fresh US tariffs. SAP surged 6% after reporting better-than-expected quarterly revenue, supported by robust cloud backlog growth and strong demand for its AI platform, while Atoss Software gained 5% on solid cloud-driven earnings. In contrast, Adidas fell 3% after disappointing investors ahead of next week's earnings release. Automakers underperformed, with Volkswagen slipping 1% after reporting weaker second-quarter earnings and lowering its revenue outlook. Porsche, BMW, and Mercedes-Benz also traded lower. Meanwhile, Brent crude remained near $100 per barrel as the US continued its military campaign against Iran and the Strait of Hormuz stayed closed, while the US also imposed new tariffs on imports from 60 trading partners, including the EU.
