US stocks were lower on Wednesday amid hot inflation data and continued volatility in the AI infrastructure sector. The S&P 500 fell 0.8% and the Nasdaq 100 slipped 1.5%, while the Dow was flat. Chip producers extended this week's losses as ASML stated it could not keep up with fab demand, while Chinese competitors introduced improved products approaching the capabilities of US and Korean firms. AMD and Intel fell 3%, while Dell, SanDisk, and Palo Alto Networks sank between 3% and 7%. Among financial companies, Bank of America tanked over 7% as both its equity and fixed-income trading revenues missed estimates. Morgan Stanley was 1% higher after its equity trading revenue topped forecasts, and BlackRock edged up after pulling in more capital in its asset management business, boosting fee revenue.

The CAC 40 rose 0.4% to 7,858 on Wednesday, rebounding from the previous session to reach a one-week high as investors drew support from dovish signals from the Federal Reserve ahead of key US inflation data. New York Fed President John Williams said one further rate hike may be warranted this year but signaled no need for additional increases in 2027, while emphasizing that the Fed should focus on “underlying trends” in inflation amid price shocks linked to energy and AI. In Europe, attention will turn to speeches by ECB officials, particularly President Christine Lagarde, alongside Elderson, Buch and Schnabel. On the corporate front, STMicroelectronics climbed 1.6%, tracking a broader tech-sector rally after ASML beat earnings and revenue forecasts and issued an upbeat Q4 sales outlook. Luxury stocks also advanced, including LVMH (1.9%), Hermès (2.3%) and Kering (1.5%), shrugging off LVMH's recent report of weaker organic sales growth amid subdued demand in China.

The FTSE 100 edged higher on Wednesday, trading near a two-week high but lagging gains across continental European markets. UK defence stocks advanced, with BAE Systems gaining 0.7%, Babcock rising 1.4% and Rolls-Royce trading 1% higher. Mining shares were mixed, as Glencore added 1.4% and Anglo American rose more than 0.5%, while Rio Tinto fell by over 0.5%. Sentiment around the technology sector remained mixed after ASML Holding warned it could not fully meet demand for chipmaking equipment over the next two years, renewing concerns about the sustainability of the artificial intelligence boom. Oil prices also continued to climb as renewed attacks in the Middle East cast doubt on the recovery of energy shipments from the Persian Gulf.

European stocks traded higher on Wednesday, recovering from losses in the previous session, with the STOXX 50 gaining 0.7% and the STOXX 600 rising 0.4%. Gains were led by the technology sector following upbeat results from ASML. The company's shares advanced 2.2% after it beat earnings and revenue expectations and issued a strong sales forecast for the fourth quarter. ASML also said that AI-driven demand for its chipmaking equipment was so strong that it would be unable to fully meet customer demand in 2027 or 2028, adding that it was exploring ways to expand capacity. Infineon Technologies rose 1.5% and STMicroelectronics gained 0.7%. Luxury stocks also performed well, with LVMH climbing 2.1%, Hermes adding 2.3% and Richemont advancing 1.6%. On the downside, Admiral tumbled 3.1%, making it one of the STOXX 600's worst performer, while Orsted fell 2.7% after Bloomberg reported that the US would reassess the permit for its Rhode Island offshore wind farm.
