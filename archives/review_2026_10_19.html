US stocks were mixed as a rebound in heavyweight AI infrastructure stocks offset muted movement in broader sectors. Tech gains lifted the S&P 500 by 0.5% and the Nasdaq 100 by 1.2%, while the Dow inched below the flatline. The advance contrasted with tech weakness last week, when underwhelming financial results from Anthropic and signs of debt stress among cloud providers, especially CoreWeave, dented demand for speculative positions in the sector. The credit stress in the sector had also softened concerns that the US Treasury would be further crowded out of capital by primary dealers, lowering longer-term yields. Banks were muted following their earnings week, while Meta and Tesla inched down ahead of their earnings results this week.

The CAC 40 fell 1.1% to close at 7,868 on Monday amid civil unrest and deepening political turmoil. Calls for higher public spending come as France faces mounting pressure to rein in its growing debt burden, complicating efforts to address social grievances without worsening its fiscal position. The protests unfolded as French lawmakers debated the government’s proposed 2027 budget, which includes €43 billion in measures aimed at reducing the deficit. The unrest also comes at a critical juncture ahead of the presidential election. A recent poll showed far-right leader Marine Le Pen and hard-left leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon gaining ground ahead of the election. Both candidates are viewed as not committed to fiscal restraint. Losses were broad-based, led by financial stocks. Crédit Agricole fell 2.6%, Société Générale shed 3.4%, and BNP Paribas lost 2.5%. EssilorLuxottica dropped 1.5% ahead of its earnings release, as Morgan Stanley cut its price target amid governance concerns.

Frankfurt’s DAX 40 closed about 1.2% down at 25,320 on Monday, tracking broader weakness across European markets, pressured by rising bond yields and elevated oil prices. Violent weekend protests in France raised concerns about political and fiscal stability, prompting investors to reassess sovereign debt risks and pushing government bond yields higher. At the same time, persistent geopolitical risks kept oil prices elevated. Meanwhile, the earnings season continued to draw attention. Most sectors faced pressure, particularly banks, utilities, industrials and insurers. Deutsche Bank and Commerzbank were among the worst performers, dropping 3.2% and 2.7%, respectively. RWE (-3%) and Bayer (-2.4%) followed suit. Rheinmetall fell 2.2%, weighed down by concerns over the costs of its expansion plans. The defense group took a small stake in Rock Tech Lithium and its German lithium processing project in a deal worth €138 million.

The FTSE 100 fell more than 1% on Monday, ending a three-session winning streak as banking and mining stocks led the decline. Barclays dropped 3.5% after reports that the BoE was investigating whether its traders had misrepresented illiquid private-credit loans to understate their risk. The news weighed on the wider banking sector, with HSBC and Lloyds Banking Group losing more than 2%, NatWest falling nearly 3% and Standard Chartered declining 2.5%. Mining shares also came under pressure, as Anglo American fell 4%, Glencore slid more than 3.5%, and Rio Tinto lost almost 2%. Aerospace and defence stocks weakened, with Rolls-Royce and BAE Systems each down around 2%. Investors are now preparing for UK September inflation and retail sales data due this week. Attention is also turning to Finance Minister John Healey’s first budget speech, scheduled for next week. Meanwhile, oil prices eased from five-month highs as traders assessed Middle East tensions and regional supply resilience.

European stocks closed sharply lower on Monday as fresh civil unrest added to local political uncertainty, triggering selloffs in the bond markets of major Eurozone members. The Euro STOXX 50 fell 1% to 6,141 and the STOXX Europe 600 lost 0.9% to 639. Protests across France and Italy against rising living costs escalated into clashes with local security forces, pressuring the outlook for the countries' respective budgets to pass smoothly ahead of their elections next year, at a time where rising yields across the Eurozone had already triggered concerns of contagion in the bloc's sovereign bond market. Banks therefore led the losses, with Deutsche Bank, BNP Paribas, Santander, UniCredit, and Intesa Sanpaolo dropping over 2%. Industrials were also down, with Airbus and Rheinmetall dropping close to 3%. Meanwhile, Hermès and SAP were relatively close to the flatline ahead of their earnings this week.
