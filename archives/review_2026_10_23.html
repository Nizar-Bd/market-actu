US stocks fell on Friday, with the S&P 500 down 0.5%, the Nasdaq losing 0.2% and the Dow Jones dropping 580 points, extending the previous session’s sharp sell-off. The CAC 40 fell 0.7% to 7,758 on Friday, extending losses from the previous session and heading for a weekly decline as investors weighed a batch of corporate earnings alongside PMI data. Luxury stocks were among the laggards, with Kering plunging 11.7% to over one-year low after Q3 2026 revenue came in flat year-on-year and down about 1% on a like-for-like basis. Hermès fell 1.1% to four-year low as its quarterly results disappointed investors amid challenging conditions in China. Safran also dropped 6.7% despite surpassing Q3 revenue growth forecasts. In contrast, L’Oréal climbed 3.5% to a more than two-year high after Q3 sales beat expectations. On the economic front, France’s flash composite PMI fell to 48.4 in October from 51.1 in September, well below forecasts of 50.5. Manufacturing PMI slipped to 49.3 from 50.6, returning to contraction and hitting an 11-month low. Services PMI also fell back into contraction, dropping to 48.4 from 51.2 and missing expectations of 50.7.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 extended early losses on Friday, falling over 1% to around 25,100, its lowest since October 9. While the earnings season continues to draw investors’ attention, sentiment remained cautious amid rising oil prices due to escalating tensions in the Middle East. Also, concerns over demonstrations scheduled for Saturday in France and Italy weighed. On the data front, flash PMI figures showed that Germany’s private sector continued to expand in October, though the pace of growth slowed more than expected. Most sectors traded lower, particularly telecom, software and autos, while tech outperformed. Among individual stocks, Deutsche Telekom led the losses, falling more than 3%, followed by MTU Aero Engines, Adidas, Continental and Airbus, which declined between 1.6% and 2.6%. On the upside, Rheinmetall (2.2%), Fresenius Medical Care (1.2%), Infineon Technologies (0.9%) and Vonovia (0.9%) posted the biggest gains. For the week, the index lost around 1.5%.

The FTSE 100 traded slightly lower on Friday, extending the previous session’s 0.3% decline and heading for a weekly loss of more than 1.5%. AstraZeneca dropped over 1%, while GSK declined more than 0.5%. Retail sales also disappointed in September, falling 0.4% including fuel and 0.7% excluding it month-on-month, both worse than expected. However, upward revisions to August’s figures partly offset the latest weakness.

European stocks extended losses on Friday, with the Stoxx 50 falling around 0.5% to a more than four-month low and recording a weekly decline of over 2%. Safran lost 10% despite higher sales, whereas L’Oréal climbed over 5% on stronger-than-expected third-quarter results.
