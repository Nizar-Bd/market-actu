US stock indices fell on Monday as higher oil prices added to inflation risks and consolidated concerns of an incoming rate hike by the Federal Reserve. The S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, and Dow fell over 0.5%. Wholesale prices for oil and products gained traction after the US and Iran delivered fresh strikes against each other. The developments followed their respective signals of blockades for tankers and vessels in the Persian Gulf, prolonging the suspension of exports from the region. Yields across the curve rose and equities for credit-sensitive sectors were under pressure. Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon dropped more than 1%. Banks and credit providers also dropped with Citi, Morgan Stanley, and Amex losing 1%. Meanwhile, Nvidia was flat after announcing a $3.5 billion stake in MediaTek, increasing its net of exposure in the global chip production and AI infrastructure. The S&P 500 gained 2.5% on August, while the Nasdaq 100 jumped 3.8% and the Dow rose 1.4%.

The CAC 40 fell 0.8% to close at 8,334 on Monday amid renewed inflationary pressures stemming from the US-Iran war. Oil prices rebounded as the US and Iran exchanged strikes for the first time in roughly a month, while fresh concerns over shipping through the Strait of Hormuz added to supply risks. Higher crude prices weighed on the index amid concerns over energy-driven inflation. Luxury stocks declined as risk aversion increased, with LVMH losing 1.1%, L’Oreal shedding 1%, and Hermes retreating 1.1%. Financials were mixed, with AXA down 1.2%. Industrials underperformed, led by Airbus (-2.9%) and Safran (-2.1%). Fitch Ratings affirmed France’s rating at ‘A+’ with a Stable Outlook. However, high and rising public debt, a challenging political and social environment, and weak potential growth are seen as constraints. Tech stocks tracked losses in the US, with Schneider Electric down 2.4% and STMicroelectronics losing 0.8%. In contrast, TotalEnergies gained 1.1% on higher oil prices.

The FTSE 100 rose 0.3% on Friday, joining a broader European rally after Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh struck a cautious tone on inflation at the Jackson Hole symposium. Warsh said recent PCE and CPI readings were encouraging but had not provided enough evidence that underlying inflation was improving decisively, reinforcing expectations that the Fed may need to keep policy restrictive. As a result, market pricing for a September rate hike increased. Financial stocks were among the strongest performers, with HSBC and Standard Chartered both gaining around 1.7%, while Barclays and Lloyds Banking Group also advanced. Utilities also supported the index, led by SSE, which climbed 2.6%, while Severn Trent, Centrica and National Grid posted more modest gains. Shell rose slightly. On the downside, defence stocks BAE Systems and Babcock International declined sharply. Despite Friday’s advance, the FTSE 100 ended August about 0.5% lower, breaking a four-month winning streak.
