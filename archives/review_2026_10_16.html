The CAC 40 fell 0.2% to 7,922 on Friday, retreating after two consecutive sessions of gains as investors grew cautious over renewed trade tensions between the EU and China. The bloc is considering new powers to restrict access to its single market and assessing import data for potential surges, a preliminary step toward imposing quotas on additional goods from China and other economies. While China has warned of retaliation against new EU trade measures or discriminatory restrictions targeting Chinese companies and products, Beijing has also signaled its willingness to work with the bloc to address global imbalances. Investors also continued to assess the outlook for the artificial intelligence sector following a recent sell-off driven by concerns over AI spending. Among the biggest decliners, L’Oréal fell 0.9%, LVMH dropped 1.2%, Schneider Electric lost 1%, and Hermès slipped 0.9%. Despite Friday’s retreat, the index remained on track to snap a two-week losing streak.

Frankfurt’s DAX 40 rose 0.6% to above 25,500 on Friday, reaching a more than three-week high and extending its gains for a third consecutive session. On the losing side, Infineon (-0.6%) and Beiersdorf (-0.4%) lagged.

The FTSE 100 traded slightly lower on Friday, lagging other European markets as concerns over potential tax increases weighed on UK banking shares. Reports that Chancellor John Healey plans to raise taxes on banks in his upcoming budget put pressure on the sector, with Lloyds falling 2.5%, NatWest dropping 2% and Barclays losing 0.6%. Lloyds also faced pressure after RBC Capital Markets downgraded its rating to sector-perform from outperform. Ithaca Energy declined more than 2% after CEO Luciano Vasques announced plans to retire by the end of March 2027, prompting the board to begin its search for a successor. Pharmaceutical stocks weakened, with AstraZeneca down 1.4% and GSK losing 1%, while Rio Tinto fell more than 1%. Defence stocks offered some support, as BAE Systems rose 0.9% and Rolls-Royce gained 0.6%. Also, British American Tobacco added 0.7%. Despite Friday’s weakness, the FTSE 100 remained on track for a weekly gain of around 2.5%, following last week’s 0.9% rise.

European stocks rose on Friday, with the STOXX 50 gaining 0.6% and the STOXX 600 advancing 0.3% to trade near two-week highs, as concerns over AI spending amid a high-interest-rate environment eased and the previous session’s tech sell-off subsided. The technology sector rebounded into positive territory following losses in the previous session. ASML Holding added 2.1%. Traders were also keeping a close eye on fresh corporate updates. Airbus (0.6%), Siemens Energy (1.1%) and Rheinmetall (3.3%) were higher and the banking sector was among the biggest gainers, including ING (1.7%) and Intesa Sanpaolo (0.8%). In contrast, LVMH (-1.1%), Novartis (-1%), and AstraZeneca (-1.5%) were in the red. Schneider Electric fell 1.1% although the company raised its 2026 core profit and revenue targets. For the week, the STOXX 50 was down 0.9%, while the STOXX 600 gained 1.4%, putting the latter on track for its strongest weekly advance since August.
