The CAC 40 fell 0.4% to 7,834 on Tuesday, extending losses from the previous session as renewed selling in global technology and AI stocks weighed on investor sentiment. Pressure on the sector persisted ahead of earnings reports from major technology companies this week, amid concerns over mounting AI investment and elevated interest rates. In France, investors also monitored ongoing social unrest as the government grappled with widespread discontent over inadequate public services and the rising cost of living. Protests turned violent over the weekend, adding to political uncertainty ahead of the presidential election in April. Among individual stocks, STMicroelectronics fell 2.8%, tracking broader losses across the technology sector. Other notable decliners included Safran (-1.5%), Legrand (-3.7%) and Société Générale (-0.9%). In contrast, EssilorLuxottica rose 1.6% ahead of its earnings later today, with revenue expected to increase despite smart eyewear challenges.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 fell nearly 1% to trade below 25,100, its lowest since October 8, extending the previous session's losses, led by tech, industrials and banks. Market sentiment remained weak amid rising oil prices and mounting concerns over the economic and political repercussions of widespread protests across several European countries. Renewed pressure on AI-related stocks added to the unease, with investors increasingly questioning the sector’s lofty valuations. Siemens, Infineon Technologies, Hochtief and Siemens Energy ranked among the worst performers, with losses ranging between 2.5% and 3.7%. Rheinmetall (-1.9%), Brenntag (-1.3%), MTU Aero Engines (-1.2%) and Vonovia (-1.2%) also struggled. Deutsche Bank fell 1.1%. On the upside, SAP led gains, rising 1.4%, followed by Fresenius Medical Care (1.2%) and Adidas (0.9%).

The FTSE 100 traded slightly lower on Tuesday after falling 1.1% in the previous session. Banking stocks remained under pressure, with HSBC, Lloyds Banking Group, NatWest and Standard Chartered down between 0.9% and 1.4%. Aerospace and defence shares also weakened, as Rolls-Royce and BAE Systems each lost more than 1%. Distribution company Bunzl was among the worst performers, falling over 3%, while Rio Tinto declined more than 1%. In contrast, Anglo American gained around 2% after raising its annual diamond production forecast as it pursues a sale of its De Beers unit. Official figures showed UK payroll employment increased by 13,000 in September, beating expectations of an 8,000 decline, while August’s reading was revised from a 26,000 drop to a 1,000 increase. The unemployment rate also eased to 4.7% in the three months to August, its lowest since July 2025, suggesting the labour market remains resilient ahead of Chancellor John Healey’s budget next week.
