commodity/brent-crude-oil 2026-01-30
https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil

Brent crude oil futures fell to below $69 per barrel on Friday but remained on track for their best month since July 2023, supported by a rising geopolitical risk premium. Concerns persist on renewed US–Iran tensions, after US President Donald Trump called on Iran to engage in nuclear talks, while Tehran warned of retaliation. Market attention is focused on the potential impact of these tensions on shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow passage between Iran and the Arabian Peninsula that is critical for global energy flows, with tankers transporting crude oil and LNG passing through it daily. Earlier this month, oil prices were also supported by geopolitical tensions in Venezuela, production outages in Kazakhstan, US production freeze-offs, and tightening US restrictions on purchases of Russian oil, factors that have pushed prices higher so far this year despite expectations of oversupply.

commodity/gold 2026-01-30
https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/gold

Gold slid more than 7% to below $4,980 per ounce on Friday, extending its pullback from the previous session on profit-taking, but remaining on track for a monthly gain of over 15%, its strongest performance since the 1980s. The recent rally was supported by elevated economic and geopolitical uncertainty, along with weakness in the US dollar. In the latest development, President Donald Trump signed an executive order imposing tariffs on goods from countries supplying oil to Cuba, a move that adds pressure on Mexico. Geopolitical tensions also remained high as Trump urged Iran to engage in nuclear talks, while Tehran warned of retaliation, vowing a swift response. On the monetary policy front, Trump said he will reveal his nominee for Federal Reserve chair on Friday, with reports indicating former Fed governor Kevin Warsh as the leading contender, who has long been a critic of ultra-loose Fed policy.

commodity/eu-natural-gas 2026-01-30
https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/eu-natural-gas

European natural gas futures fell more than 3% to around €38.5/MWh on Friday, as improving supply conditions pressured prices, even as the contract remained sharply higher for the month. US LNG flows recovered after storm disruptions earlier in the week, with milder conditions allowing export plants to increase output again. That helped reduce immediate supply anxiety for Europe, which relies heavily on US LNG. Even so, prices were still set for a roughly 37% gain in January, the strongest monthly rise since June 2023, driven by prolonged cold weather and fast storage withdrawals. European gas inventories are about 43% full, near the lowest level for this time of year since 2022, highlighting a fragile supply balance. January’s rally was driven by temporary US production outages during winter storms and broader concerns over global supplies. Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, particularly uncertainty around Iran, also kept risk premiums elevated.

france/stock-market 2026-01-30
https://tradingeconomics.com/france/stock-market

The CAC 40 rose 0.2% to 8,090 on Friday, extending gains from the previous session as investors absorbed a fresh round of economic data from across Europe. Domestically, preliminary data showed that the French economy expanded by 0.2% quarter-on-quarter in Q4 2025, in line with market expectations but marking a slowdown from the 0.5% growth recorded in Q3. In 2025, GDP grew by 0.9%, matching the pace assumed by the government in its budget projections. Meanwhile, Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu invoked special constitutional powers on Friday to push the 2026 budget through the divided lower house without a vote, a move likely to prompt further no-confidence motions. Notable performers included LVMH and Hermès, both up 0.5%, Schneider Electric edged up 0.2%, and Safran advanced 0.5%. Sanofi also gained 0.7% after reporting a 13% increase in Q4 sales and forecasting high-single-digit revenue growth for 2026. Over the month, the CAC 40 is on track to post a monthly decline.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 rose 0.8% to surpass 24,500 on Friday, bouncing back after three consecutive sessions of declines. The FTSE 100 traded near the flatline on Friday as falling metals and oil prices weighed on heavyweight miners and energy stocks, offsetting gains in banking shares. euro-area/stock-market 2026-01-30
https://tradingeconomics.com/euro-area/stock-market

European stocks edged higher on the final trading day of January, with the STOXX 50 rising 0.5% and the STOXX 600 adding 0.2%, rebounding after losses in the previous two sessions. Investors remained focused on corporate earnings while digesting key economic data. Adidas shares jumped 4% after the company reported a 13% increase in 2025 revenues, reaching a record high. CaixaBank gained nearly 3% following a 1.8% rise in net profit, while Electrolux surged more than 16% after posting a stronger-than-expected fourth-quarter operating profit. Other notable gainers included SAP (+3.6%), UniCredit (+1.4%) and BBVA (+1.4%). In contrast, mining stocks lagged, with Anglo American down 2.7%, Glencore falling 2.7% and Rio Tinto slipping 2.5%. On the data front, Eurozone GDP grew 0.3% in Q4, above forecasts of 0.2%, with Germany’s economy expanding 0.3% for the first time in three quarters. For January as a whole, the STOXX 50 advanced 1.9%, while the STOXX 600 gained 2.4%.
