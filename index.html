US equities extended their mixed momentum as pressure on AI infrastructure offset resilience in traditional sectors of the economy. The S&P 500 fell 0.5% and the Nasdaq 100 dropped 1.5%, its sixth decline in seven sessions. Chip producers and AI hyperscalers sank further after Anthropic disclosed third-quarter sales figures that slowed from the pace earlier this year and missed most estimates, reinforcing skepticism that soaring expenditure in the sector may not translate into returns. Micron, Intel, AMD, and Marvell dropped over 6%, while Seagate and Western Digital tumbled 9%. On top of that, hot producer inflation consolidated bets of a Fed hike this year to add stress on datacenter operators that took record-levels of debt to finance capex, with CoreWeave sinking 8% as its bonds declined sharply. Still, the Dow gained 0.6% as a rally in Treasuries exemplified the pivot from speculative companies to established sectors. Walmart added 3% and Eli Lilly gained 2%.

The CAC 40 rose 0.3% to 7,896 on Thursday, extending gains from the previous session despite a deepening global selloff in technology and artificial intelligence stocks. The sector came under pressure as concerns over AI-related valuations and the sustainability of heavy infrastructure spending weighed on sentiment. Inflation also remained in focus following stronger-than-expected US data. Domestically, annual inflation rate was revised up to 3.1% in September 2026 from a preliminary estimate of 3.0%, accelerating from 2.4% in August and reaching its highest level since January 2024. On the corporate front, Pernod Ricard climbed 3.4% after reporting a smaller-than-expected 0.9% decline in first-quarter fiscal 2027 sales, as weak demand and destocking in China and the United States were partly offset by strong demand in India. On the downside, Schneider Electric fell 5% and STMicroelectronics plunged 7.2%, making them among the session’s worst performers.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 fell about 0.4% to trade near 25,200 on Thursday, pressured by a broader sell-off in technology stocks. Investor caution intensified as concerns over AI-related spending coincided with fears of higher US interest rates and the prospect of increased financing costs, particularly for companies reliant on substantial investment. Meanwhile, traders continued to monitor geopolitical developments and corporate earnings worldwide. Infineon Technologies plunged over 9%, making it the worst performer, with AI-related Siemens Energy (-6.9%) and Hochtief (-3.5%) also falling sharply. Other notable decliners included Siemens, Continental, GEA Group, RWE, Deutsche Bank and Commerzbank, falling between 0.6% and 2.6%. On the upside, telecom, insurers and software companies advanced the most. Deutsche Telekom led gains, rising over 3%, followed by Scout24 (3%), Hannover Ruck (2.6%) and Munchener Ruck (2.5%).

The FTSE 100 rose to a four-week high on Thursday, outperforming major European benchmarks as stronger-than-expected UK growth data supported sentiment and the index’s limited exposure to struggling technology stocks provided a relative advantage. GDP expanded by 0.1% in August, following July’s 0.4% increase and exceeding expectations for a 0.1% contraction. Output grew 0.7% over the three months to August, marking the fourth consecutive month in which economic performance surpassed forecasts despite high borrowing costs and rising energy prices. Among individual stocks, consumer and pharmaceutical companies advanced, with Unilever gaining 1.5%, British American Tobacco rising 2.8%, AstraZeneca adding 1% and GSK climbing 0.8%. Mining shares, however, weighed on the market. Antofagasta plunged more than 6% after quarterly copper production missed estimates and its 2027 output forecast was reduced. Anglo American fell 5%, Rio Tinto dropped over 4% and Glencore lost more than 3%.

European stocks fell on Thursday, with the STOXX 50 down 0.7% and the STOXX 600 slipping 0.3%, tracking losses across US and Asian markets amid a global tech sell-off. Chip stocks tumbled on concerns over AI-related valuations, while high US inflation, prospects of high interest rates and elevated oil prices added to investor caution. Markets also kept a close eye on fresh corporate earnings. ASML Holding plunged 6.6%, while Infineon and STMicroelectronics dropped 9.2% and 7.3%, respectively, among the STOXX 600’s worst performers. Basic resources and industrial goods stocks also came under pressure, with Siemens falling 2.7%, Schneider Electric losing 5.1%, ABB declining 4.6%, and Siemens Energy dropping 7.7%. In contrast, telecoms and household goods stocks advanced. Ericsson led the STOXX 600, surging nearly 10% after returning to sales growth in Q3 and reporting better-than-expected earnings. Pernod Ricard added 3.4% after a smaller-than-expected sales decline last quarter.
