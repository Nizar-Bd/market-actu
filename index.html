commodity/brent-crude-oil 2026-01-23

Brent crude oil futures rose toward $65 per barrel on Friday, recovering some losses from the previous session, as renewed threats from President Trump against Iran stoked supply concerns. Trump said an “armada” is heading to Iran but expressed hope it would not be needed, warning Tehran against targeting protesters or restarting its nuclear program, raising the risk of military action in OPEC’s fourth-largest producer. Meanwhile, Saudi Aramco’s CEO downplayed concerns about a global oil glut, citing strong underlying demand, with total consumption reaching record levels last year and expected to rise further in 2026. This comes after the International Energy Agency reiterated that global oil supply is likely to exceed demand this year, even after slightly revising its demand growth forecast upward. Oil was further supported by a softer dollar, which boosted the appeal of dollar-denominated commodities.

commodity/eu-natural-gas 2026-01-22
https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/eu-natural-gas

European natural gas futures fell more than 4% to around €37.9 per megawatt hour, pulling back from a 12% rally over the previous two sessions as geopolitical risk premiums eased. President Donald Trump said he would refrain from imposing tariffs on European countries opposing his plans to take control of Greenland, reducing fears of an escalation in US EU trade tensions. Trump said a framework for a future deal had been discussed following a meeting with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte, although no details were provided. The EU sourced 27% of its total gas and LNG imports from the US in 2025, up from 6% in 2021. Despite the pullback, market conditions remain tight as US gas flows have been diverted from LNG export facilities to meet strong domestic demand during an Arctic blast, while colder weather in Asia has intensified competition for LNG cargoes. In Europe, gas storage has fallen to about 48.4% full, well below roughly 59.3% a year ago.

france/stock-market 2026-01-23
https://tradingeconomics.com/france/stock-market

The CAC 40 edged down 0.1% on Friday, on track to end a volatile week lower by around 1.5% at 8,140 points, as investors assessed the potential fallout from ongoing trade tensions linked to Greenland. The week opened with US President Donald Trump threatening to impose tariffs on eight European countries, including France, unless Washington is allowed to purchase Greenland. Although Trump later withdrew the threat, citing the possibility of a Greenland agreement with NATO, investor caution persisted amid concerns that tariffs could be used as a negotiating tool. Elsewhere, markets digested fresh flash PMI data and business survey results from France. Private sector activity unexpectedly contracted in January, as weakness in services more than offset an improvement in manufacturing. On a more positive note, France’s manufacturing business climate indicator improved to its strongest level since July 2022.

germany/stock-market 2026-01-23
https://tradingeconomics.com/germany/stock-market

The DAX 40 hovered around 24,860 on Friday, following a solid rebound yesterday after President Trump signaled a softer stance on Greenland. Investors remained cautious amid broader uncertainty stemming from lingering geopolitical tensions and decisions by the US administration, with negotiations for an end to the war in Ukraine showing renewed momentum. Meanwhile, traders assessed fresh economic data, including PMI surveys, and corporate earnings. Domestically, both manufacturing and services PMIs for Germany topped expectations in January, underpinning an acceleration in private-sector activity. Defense names like Hensoldt (2.8%), Renk (1.7%) and Rheinmetall (1.3%) were among the best performers. Other top advancers included Siemens Energy (2.2%), SAP (1.9%) and Bayer (1.5%). On the downside, BASF dropped 1.8% after the German chemical group reported preliminary earnings that missed both its guidance and market expectations. For the week, the index was set to decline about 1.7%.

euro-area/stock-market 2026-01-23
https://tradingeconomics.com/euro-area/stock-market

The STOXX 50 fell 0.5% and the STOXX 600 slipped 0.3% on Friday, following a subdued open, as traders paused after gains of nearly 1% in the previous session. Markets continued to digest the latest pivot from US President Trump regarding Greenland and additional tariff measures, while also assessing a fresh batch of corporate earnings. Ericsson shares surged 12% after the company reported a profit beat, with Nokia (+3.1%) and Novo Nordisk (+3%) also among the top performers. On the downside, Babcock (-1.3%) and Maersk (-2.8%) were among the biggest laggards, while BASF slipped 1.7% after reporting a projected profit decline for 2025. On the data front, preliminary PMIs for the Eurozone showed a surprise slowdown in the services sector and France’s services sector unexpectedly fell into contraction while figures for Germany exceeded expectations. For the week, the STOXX 50 fell 1.2% and the STOXX 600 declined 0.9%, snapping their longest streak of weekly gains since May.
