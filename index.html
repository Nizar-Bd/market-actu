Brent crude oil futures climbed toward $68 per barrel on Wednesday, extending gains from the previous session as geopolitical tensions resurfaced after the US downed an Iranian drone near a US aircraft carrier in the Arabian Sea. The incident unsettled energy markets, though President Donald Trump emphasized that diplomatic channels remain open, with the White House confirming US-Iran talks are still scheduled for Friday. Adding to tensions, Iranian vessels challenged a US-flagged oil tanker transiting the Strait of Hormuz, with US Central Command saying the IRGC harassed the Stena Imperative. Further support came from API data showing US crude inventories fell by 11.1 million barrels last week, the largest draw since June if confirmed by official figures. Meanwhile, OPEC+ expects oil demand to gradually pick up from March or April and will decide on March 1 whether to resume monthly output increases after a first-quarter pause.

commodity/eu-natural-gas 2026-02-03
https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/eu-natural-gas

European natural gas futures fell to around €33 per megawatt hour, moving further away from a seven-month high of €40 hit on January 23, as concerns about LNG supply eased. Near-term US weather forecasts turned milder, reducing heating demand and freeing up more gas for LNG exports. This improved the outlook for deliveries to Europe, which now relies on LNG for roughly half of its gas needs after losing most Russian pipeline supplies. The US supplied about 27% of the EU’s gas and LNG imports in 2025, making European prices highly sensitive to US supply conditions. Geopolitical risk premiums also faded after President Donald Trump said the US is in talks with Iran, easing fears of disruptions to LNG and oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. Still, EU gas storage is at just 41.1% full, far below last year’s level and the five-year average, keeping the market vulnerable to renewed cold or supply shocks.

united-states/stock-market 2026-02-03

US stock futures steadied on Wednesday after technology shares dragged major indexes lower in the previous session, as investors assessed a fresh batch of earnings. In extended trading, Advanced Micro Devices sank more than 8% after issuing a weaker-than-expected Q1 forecast, while Chipotle slid about 6% as traffic fell for a fourth straight quarter. In contrast, Match Group jumped 7% on stronger-than-expected earnings and revenue. During regular trading on Tuesday, the Dow fell 0.34%, the S&P 500 lost 0.84%, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.43%, as investors rotated out of richly valued technology stocks into more cyclical names. The selloff was concentrated in AI and semiconductor shares, with notable declines in Nvidia (-2.8%), Microsoft (-2.9%), Micron (-4.2%), Broadcom (-3.3%), and Oracle (-3.4%). Attention now turns to Alphabet’s earnings on Wednesday, followed by Amazon on Thursday.

france/stock-market 2026-02-04
https://tradingeconomics.com/france/stock-market

The CAC 40 rose 0.6% to 8,226 on Wednesday, tracking gains across most European peers as investors positioned ahead of key macroeconomic releases, including the Eurozone flash CPI and the US ADP employment report. Preliminary inflation data from the euro area is expected to shape market sentiment ahead of Thursday’s European Central Bank interest rate decision, with policymakers closely monitoring the euro’s recent strength. In France, investors are also awaiting final PMI readings for further insight into the health of the domestic economy. Among individual stocks, Hermès (0.6%), Schneider Electric (1.4%), and TotalEnergies (1.8%) recorded gains. In contrast, Credit Agricole fell 3%, making it one of the session’s worst performers. France’s third-largest listed lender reported a 39% year-on-year decline in Q4 net profit, weighed down by a charge linked to its increased stake in Italy’s Banco BPM, alongside rising costs and losses in its car-leasing business.

The FTSE 100 rose more than 0.5% on Wednesday, climbing to a fresh record above 10,380 after a 0.3% decline in the previous session, driven by energy and mining stocks. Oil majors led gains, with Shell and BP up around 2% to 2.3%, as crude prices advanced for a second session amid rising geopolitical tensions between the United States and Iran. Mining shares also edged higher as gold and silver extended their rebound, lifting Fresnillo by nearly 2.5% and Endeavour by 1.7%, while Rio Tinto gained 1%, Glencore rose 1.4% and Anglo American added 1.1%. Copper was flat to slightly lower, limiting gains in Antofagasta. On the downside, stocks seen as vulnerable to artificial intelligence disruption continued to fall, with Relx down 2%, London Stock Exchange Group lower by 1.4% and WPP down 2.2%, extending losses from the previous session as investors reassessed risks to their business models.

euro-area/stock-market 2026-02-04
https://tradingeconomics.com/euro-area/stock-market

European equities gained on Wednesday, with the STOXX 50 rising 0.4% above the 6,000 level and the broader STOXX 600 climbing 0.2% to a record 619 points, supported by signs of easing inflation across the eurozone. January’s CPI slowed to 1.7%, comfortably below the ECB’s target, potentially fueling concerns among policymakers over the euro’s rapid appreciation and increasing the likelihood of a restart of rate cuts halted in June. Tech and media shares remained under pressure as investors assessed the impact of new AI-related tools on traditional business models. On the corporate front, Novo Nordisk plunged 17% after issuing a disappointing forecast for its weight-loss drug, while Credit Agricole fell more than 2% following a Q4 profit miss. UBS dropped 5% despite a 56% jump in net profit, and Santander slipped 3% after announcing its $12.2 billion acquisition of US regional lender Webster Financial.
