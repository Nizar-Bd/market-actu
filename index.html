The CAC 40 rose 1% to close at 7,970 on Thursday, reaching its highest level in a month after the ECB left interest rates unchanged, as expected. Policymakers maintained the deposit rate at 2.5% as they assessed inflation risks stemming from energy market disruptions following the US-Iran war. However, ECB officials remain prepared to raise rates in December if the inflation outlook fails to improve. France’s 10-year OAT yield slipped after the policy meeting. Gains were broad-based amid the favorable backdrop and a fresh batch of corporate earnings. Société Générale rose 0.7% after beating earnings and revenue estimates. TotalEnergies gained 1.2% after exceeding third-quarter earnings expectations, supported by higher oil prices amid the Middle East conflict. Renault climbed 6.9% on resilient third-quarter revenue, driven by a surge in electric vehicle sales. Sanofi and Crédit Agricole, up 0.8% and 0.3%, respectively, are due to report on Friday.

Frankfurt's DAX 40 extended early gains to close about 1.3% firmer at 26,053 on Thursday, its highest since late August and notching a fourth session of advances. Investors parsed a fresh raft of corporate earnings while digesting the ECB’s latest monetary policy decision. The central bank maintained the status quo, as expected, adopting a wait-and-see approach and noting no second-round effects from higher energy prices so far. Most sectors gained, led by consumer goods, banks, pharma & healthcare, industrials and tech. Adidas climbed nearly 6%, making it the top performer, buoyed by record profits and an upgraded full-year outlook. Deutsche Bank also advanced solidly, rising 3.8%, alongside Bayer (4.1%) and Merck (2.9%). Rheinmetall gained 3.3% as it won option order for 69 Schakal infantry fighting vehicles. On the flip side, Vonovia (-3%) remained at the bottom, as it adopted a more cautious full-year outlook and revised some 2028 targets following preliminary nine-months results.

European stocks closed higher on Thursday, with the STOXX 50 rising 0.8% and the STOXX 600 gaining 0.9%, rebounding from a lackluster morning session. Investors assessed fresh corporate earnings while digesting the ECB’s latest monetary policy decision. As widely expected, the central bank left interest rates unchanged, noting that the full effects of the energy shock were still unfolding, while underlying inflation had remained broadly stable and wages had yet to show a material response. Shell added 1.7% after nearly doubling its earnings and profits. Adidas rose 5.8% after reporting record profits and raising its full-year outlook. Renault surged more than 7% after maintaining its full-year outlook. Volkswagen also rebounded, gaining 1.7% despite reporting a sharp operating loss for the quarter. On the downside, BBVA fell 2.7% after reporting lower profits in its domestic market, while Lloyds dropped 5.4% as its results disappointed investors.
