The CAC 40 fell 1.2% to 7,830 on Thursday, reversing gains from the previous session as rising French borrowing costs and political uncertainty weighed on investor confidence. France’s 10-year government bond yield climbed further after surpassing 5% for the first time since 2002, reflecting concerns over the government’s ability to restore public finances amid ongoing political turmoil and planned protests. Investors are also awaiting Moody’s review of France’s Aa3 sovereign credit rating on October 23, with its negative outlook raising the risk of a downgrade that could further increase borrowing costs. On the broader market, European stocks also fell as disappointing results from major US technology companies revived concerns over the profitability of heavy AI investments. Losses in Paris were led by Hermès International, which dropped 7.3%, followed by STMicroelectronics (-3.2%) and Kering (-3.1%).

Frankfurt's DAX 40 fell more than 1% to trade below 25,400 on Thursday, retreating from one-month highs hit in the prior session. The decline mirrored broader weakness across European markets as escalating tensions in the Middle East drove oil prices higher, while mixed earnings from major US technology companies reignited concerns over elevated AI valuations. Meanwhile, investors also closely monitored earnings from European companies. Most sectors came under pressure, led by technology stocks, while losses in banking, industrials and utilities also weighed on the market. Infineon Technologies and Siemens Energy were among the top losers, each falling over 3%. In contrast, SAP shares rose 0.5% after quarterly revenue exceeded €10 billion for the first time, supported by 26% growth in cloud revenue, prompting the company to raise its full-year revenue outlook. Other top decliners included Siemens, Deutsche Bank, Bayer, Commerzbank and MTU Aero Engines, falling between 1.9%-2.5%.

The FTSE 100 fell more than 0.5% on Thursday amid a broad equity selloff, with disappointing corporate updates from Barclays and J Sainsbury adding pressure. Barclays shares dropped over 2% after third-quarter investment banking revenue came in below expectations at £3.13 billion, despite strength in equities, prompting the bank to announce a £500 million share buyback. Analysts flagged weakness in fixed-income revenue as a concern. Sainsbury’s declined more than 3% despite reporting first-half adjusted profit of £260 million, above estimates, as investors assessed its revised outlook following the agreed sale of Argos to Swift Partners. Mining stocks also slumped as gold, silver and copper prices fell sharply, with Fresnillo down nearly 4%, Anglo American losing more than 3.5%, and Endeavour and Antofagasta falling close to 3%. Oil prices moved higher as renewed Middle East hostilities and deadly attacks in Saudi Arabia heightened concerns over regional energy supplies.
