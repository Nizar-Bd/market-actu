Stocks in the US were lower on Friday, with the S&P 500 losing 0.3%, the Nasdaq tumbling 2.4% and the Dow Jones decreasing 185 points, weighed by a sell-off in the tech sector, following disappointing results from Amazon and Intel. The CAC 40 edged up 0.1% to 7,978 on Friday, reaching its highest level in a month and putting the benchmark on track for a modest monthly gain despite mounting economic headwinds, particularly an unexpected economic contraction and a fresh acceleration in inflation. Preliminary estimates showed France’s GDP contracted 0.1% q-o-q in Q3 2026, following stagnation in the previous quarter and missing expectations for 0.1% growth. Meanwhile, annual inflation accelerated to 3.4% in October from 3.1% in September, exceeding forecasts of 3.2% and marking its highest level since December 2023. The data added to mounting economic and political pressures, including rising borrowing costs, violent protests and persistent fiscal uncertainty. L’Oréal (1.3%), Hermès (1.3%) and Air Liquide (1.2%) led the gainers. In contrast, Schneider Electric (-3%), BNP Paribas (-1.9%) and Safran (-1.1%) lagged. STMicroelectronics also tumbled 3.8%, tracking broader weakness across the technology sector.

Frankfurt's DAX traded 0.2% down on Friday, halting a four-day winning streak, pressured by rising oil prices and tech weakness. Investor enthusiasm for artificial intelligence faced another test, as disappointing earnings from Amazon and Intel triggered a broader sell-off that swept through European technology stocks. Meanwhile, market participants braced for a deluge of economic data, particularly inflation and GDP, while also eyeing more corporate earnings. Siemens Energy, Infineon Technologies, Hochtief were among the worst performers, with losses ranging between 2%-5%. On the upside, Bayer shares surged 4% after securing a unanimous jury verdict in its favour in a lawsuit alleging that its Roundup weedkiller caused cancer in three women, offering a potential boost to its efforts to contain mounting litigation risks. The benchmark index was on track to post gains of more than 3% over both the week and the month.
