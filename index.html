US stocks were higher on Wednesday, with the S&P 500 rising 0.4%, the Nasdaq gaining 0.9% and the Dow Jones increasing 75 points, supported by upbeat results from SpaceX and Super Micro Computer that revived optimism around AI demand. AT&T dropped 6.4% despite beating earnings estimates, while Elevance Health was little changed despite raising its 2026 profit outlook.

The CAC 40 rose 0.3% to 7,859 on Wednesday, rebounding from two consecutive sessions of losses as investors monitored France’s fiscal and political outlook alongside corporate earnings. Finance Minister Roland Lescure expressed confidence that France would pass next year’s budget, citing constitutional mechanisms available if parliament fails to reach an agreement. However, securing a compromise remains challenging amid deep political divisions and the presidential election just six months away. On the corporate front, EssilorLuxottica soared 11.8% after the Ray-Ban maker beat Q3 revenue expectations on resilient eyewear demand. STMicroelectronics also gained 1.6%, tracking broader technology-sector strength after solid earnings from major US tech companies eased concerns over stretched AI valuations. In contrast, Michelin slumped 4.7% after reporting lower nine-month sales as declining tyre volumes outweighed improved currency effects in Q3.

The FTSE 100 edged higher on Wednesday, recovering from two sessions of losses but underperforming other European markets. Oil majors provided support, with BP gaining around 2%, while HSBC, Lloyds Banking Group, NatWest and Standard Chartered advanced between 0.5% and 1.1%. Barclays slipped slightly ahead of its corporate results due on Thursday. Fresnillo fell more than 2% after maintaining its annual silver production outlook despite lower output compared with last year. Investors also assessed fresh UK economic data showing persistent inflationary pressure and rising public borrowing. Annual consumer price inflation accelerated to 3.5% in September, above expectations of 3.4%, potentially strengthening the case for tighter monetary policy from the Bank of England. Meanwhile, government borrowing reached £97.8 billion in the April–September period, exceeding the Office for Budget Responsibility’s March forecast by £6 billion, while September borrowing totalled £24.5 billion.

European stocks rebounded on Wednesday after two consecutive sessions of losses, with the Euro Stoxx 50 rising more than 1% and the broader Stoxx 600 gaining less than 1%. Germany, France, Italy and Spain all advanced by over 1%, while the UK market posted a more modest recovery. Technology shares led sector gains, climbing around 2%, followed by energy stocks, which rose more than 1%. Banking, industrial goods and services, healthcare, insurance and utilities also recorded gains of approximately 1%. Automobiles and parts was the weakest-performing sector, falling more than 1.5%. Among individual stocks, EssilorLuxottica surged over 15% after reporting third-quarter revenue that exceeded market expectations. Carrefour, by contrast, plunged more than 10% after cutting its profit outlook and reducing planned capital expenditure. The French retailer cited continued domestic unrest and an energy price shock as factors weighing on consumer confidence and complicating its business outlook.
